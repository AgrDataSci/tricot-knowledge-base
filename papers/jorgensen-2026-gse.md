## Questions this paper answers

- How is tricot being set up to test whether it works in a high-latitude country like Norway?
- How is tricot adapted to perennial forage seed mixtures?
- How can tricot be combined with RCBD trials at research stations?
- What do farmers evaluate in forage mini-trials, and when?
- Which forage species and seed mixtures are being compared, and for which regions?

## Summary

Milk and meat are Norway's most important agricultural sectors, and they depend on a stable supply of high-quality forage. Choosing species, cultivars and seed mixtures for conditions that vary widely in latitude and altitude is difficult, and climate change shifts production boundaries unpredictably. Without on-farm data, varieties or mixtures may be recommended that underperform in farmers' fields. Conventional RCBD trials are labour-intensive and costly, which limits the number of sites and usually excludes farmers from evaluation.

The paper presents the INNOFORAGE project (2025–2028), which brings tricot to Norway for the first time. Its aim is a cost-effective, farmer-led testing framework to identify forage mixtures suited to local conditions and to promote their adoption. The authors will evaluate whether tricot gives robust results across Norway's diverse climates, and how participatory research supports farmer engagement and knowledge sharing. tricot is combined with RCBD trials at a research station and selected farms, because metric traits such as feed quality and biomass yield still need controlled experiments.

Twelve seed mixtures were developed with seed companies, advisers and researchers, some common to all regions and others targeted at southern and central or northern and mountain areas. By our count from Table 1, each region draws on 10 mixtures, eight common and two regional, which matches the ten options named in the abstract. ClimMob assigned three mixtures to each mini-trial in a balanced incomplete block design. In summer 2025, 100 mini-trials were established, each with three plots of 3 by 7 m managed as part of the farmer's field. Thirty trials with northern and mountain mixtures went to farmers at about 61 to 70° N, and seventy to central and southern farmers between about 59 and 67° N.

Farmers evaluate with best and worst questions at set times. In the establishment autumn they judge sown-species and legume cover, weed invasion and plant height. In spring they report winter conditions such as ice cover. At harvest they judge yield, legume and weed proportions, and overall performance compared with the surrounding field, and report fertilizer use. Management, soil and agro-climate indices enter a confirmatory Plackett-Luce model as covariates. Primary outcomes are overall rank, winter survival and yield across mixture, region and year. Secondary outcomes such as weed invasion, plant height and legume cover are analysed in an exploratory way. Trials run for at least two production years.

Interest exceeded expectations, with more than the planned 100 farmers wanting to join. These extra farmers will join the 2026 round, when another 100 participants will be recruited, and mixtures may be adjusted from first-round feedback by farmers and advisers. Advisers were keen to use the mini-trials at field days. Farmer engagement and learning will be studied with interviews, surveys and stakeholder meetings. The paper reports no performance results. The mini-trials were being sown at the time of writing, and first results were expected in early autumn 2025.

## Methods in brief

- **Design.** 12 mixtures, 3 per farm, balanced incomplete blocks assigned in ClimMob, combined with station and on-farm RCBD trials.
- **Trials.** 100 mini-trials in 2025, plots of 3 × 7 m, at least two production years, another 100 farmers in 2026.
- **Evaluation.** Best-worst questions in the establishment autumn and at harvest, a spring report on winter conditions such as ice cover, plus overall performance against the surrounding field and fertilizer type and amount.
- **Analysis.** Plackett-Luce models with management, soil and agro-climate covariates. Mixed methods for farmer engagement.

## Stated limitations

- Feed quality and biomass yield need controlled RCBD trials alongside tricot.
- Results were not yet available at writing, with first results expected in autumn 2025.

## Notes

- There are two regional trials implemented with 12 mixtures. Table 1 (p. 518) lists 8 mixtures common to both regions (1 to 7 and 10), 2 for South/Central (8 and 11) and 2 for North/Mountain (9 and 12). Each regional pool therefore has 10 mixtures, which matches the "ten options" in the abstract (p. 516). The methods text speaks of twelve mixtures overall (p. 517).
- Outside information, not in the paper (source not recorded, possibly someone in the project): an earlier version of this note said each region has "10 forages + 1 regional check". The paper names no check mixture (pp. 516 to 518). Mixtures 8 and 9 have the same species proportions (40% timothy, 50% meadow fescue, 10% red clover) and differ only in their region label. The paper does not say whether they differ in cultivars or serve as regional checks.
- In Table 1 every mixture contains 10 to 20% legumes (card's sum). Lucerne appears only in mixture 10 (10%). Mixture 11 is 90% perennial ryegrass with 10% white clover.
- Source issue: the methods say 100 mini-trials were established in summer 2025 (p. 517), but the results say they were still being sown (p. 518). The paper was written during establishment.
- The common species names in the crops field are our translation of the Latin names in Table 1. The paper uses the newer names Lolium pratense (meadow fescue) and Lolium arundinaceum (tall fescue).
