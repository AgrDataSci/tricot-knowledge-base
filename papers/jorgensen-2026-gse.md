## Questions this paper answers

- Can tricot work in a high-income, high-latitude country like Norway?
- How is tricot adapted to perennial forage seed mixtures?
- How can tricot be combined with RCBD trials at research stations?
- What do farmers evaluate in forage mini-trials, and when?

## Summary

Milk and meat are Norway's most important agricultural sectors, and they depend on a stable supply of high-quality forage. Choosing species, cultivars and seed mixtures for conditions that vary widely in latitude and altitude is difficult, and climate change shifts production boundaries unpredictably. Without on-farm data, varieties or mixtures may be recommended that underperform in farmers' fields. Conventional RCBD trials are labour-intensive and costly, which limits the number of sites and usually excludes farmers from evaluation.

The paper presents the INNOFORAGE project (2025–2028), which brings tricot to Norway for the first time. Its aim is a cost-effective, farmer-led testing framework to identify forage mixtures suited to local conditions and to promote their adoption. Tricot is combined with RCBD trials at a research station and selected farms, because metric traits such as feed quality and biomass yield still need controlled experiments.

Twelve seed mixtures were developed with seed companies, advisers and researchers, some common to all regions and others targeted at southern and central or northern and mountain areas. ClimMob assigned three mixtures to each mini-trial in a balanced incomplete block design. In summer 2025, 100 mini-trials were established, each with three plots of 3 by 7 m managed as part of the farmer's field. Thirty trials with northern and mountain mixtures went to farmers at about 61 to 70° N, and seventy to central and southern farmers between about 59 and 67° N.

Farmers evaluate with best and worst questions at set times. In the establishment autumn they judge sown-species and legume cover, weed invasion and plant height. In spring they report winter conditions such as ice cover. At harvest they judge yield, legume and weed proportions, and overall performance compared with the surrounding field, and report fertilizer use. Management, soil and agro-climate indices enter a confirmatory Plackett-Luce model as covariates. Primary outcomes are overall rank, winter survival and yield across mixture, region and year. Trials run for at least two production years.

Interest exceeded expectations, with more than the planned 100 farmers wanting to join, so another 100 will be recruited in 2026 and mixtures may be adjusted from first-round feedback. Advisers were keen to use the mini-trials at field days. Farmer engagement and learning will be studied with interviews, surveys and stakeholder meetings.

## Methods in brief

- **Design.** 12 mixtures, 3 per farm, balanced incomplete blocks assigned in ClimMob, combined with station and on-farm RCBD trials.
- **Trials.** 100 mini-trials in 2025, plots of 3 × 7 m, at least two production years, another 100 farmers in 2026.
- **Evaluation.** Best-worst questions in autumn, spring and at harvest, plus overall performance against the surrounding field.
- **Analysis.** Plackett-Luce models with management, soil and agro-climate covariates. Mixed methods for farmer engagement.

## Stated limitations

- Feed quality and biomass yield need controlled RCBD trials alongside tricot.
- Results were not yet available at writing, with first results expected in autumn 2025.
