## Questions this paper answers

- Can tricot evaluate forage mixtures, not just single varieties?
- Which forage traits do Ethiopian farmers assess and value?
- How do improved forages and mixtures compare with the reference grass Desho?
- What enables scaling of improved forages?

## Summary

Livestock support the livelihoods of about 70% of Ethiopia's population, but about 90% of feed comes from traditional grazing, mostly on poorly managed natural pastures. Land degradation is mainly linked to the livestock sector, through overgrazing and unsustainable farming practices. Improved forages can provide better feed and help restore land, and farmers' participation in choosing them is key to adoption. The team therefore used tricot to identify how forage mixtures perform and which traits farmers prefer.

Sites were chosen in mid-altitude mixed farming systems across agroecologies with dairy potential, forage scarcity and earlier experience promoting improved forages. A map shows eight tricot woredas, and field photos show Dangla and Dera in Amhara and Bilate in Sidama. Woreda development agents were trained, and package labels are dated 2022. Each farmer received three separate options, drawn from 21 grasses, legumes and grass-legume mixtures. The grasses were Mombasa, Mulato II, Cayman and Desho, and the legumes were vetch, stylo, lablab, desmodium and crotalaria.

Farmers answered best and worst questions for 19 traits plus overall preference. These covered establishment (ease of sowing), plant height, soil cover, plant, grass and legume populations, weeds, greenness, disease, pest and drought resistance, leaf-to-stem ratio, fertilizer needs, soil retention, soil fertility improvement, yield, ease of harvesting, livestock preference and ease of transplanting. The results slides show up to 17 of these traits at each of two harvests.

Biomass (yield) results are shown separately for Amhara (n = 383) and SNNP (n = 354), as the probability of each option outperforming the reference forage Desho. The slides do not say whether n counts farmers or whether biomass was measured or ranked. In Amhara, only the four-way mixture of Mombasa, Mulato II, desmodium and vetch was above 0.5 (about 0.57). In SNNP, seven options were above 0.5, led by Mulato II and vetch (about 0.6 each), then Mombasa alone and four mixtures that contain Mombasa, including the same four-way mixture. Mulato II alone was lowest in Amhara (about 0.14). The chart shows no uncertainty intervals. Kendall correlations with overall preference were highest for ease of harvesting at the second harvest (about 0.3 to 0.5) and ease of transplanting at the first harvest (about 0.3 to 0.45). Yield was only weakly linked (about 0.1 or less). Plackett-Luce log-worth estimates on the pooled data highlighted Desho and the four-way mixture. Desho scored well on disease, pest and drought resistance, while the mixture scored well on yield and soil cover but poorly on ease of harvesting.

The lessons point to scaling mixtures to similar suitable environments, and to the value of engaging farmers in technology selection for new insights and easier scaling. The lessons slide also lists the influence of familiar varieties on preferences, and the role of biophysical and socio-economic factors and of different niches in performance and preference. Next steps include studying the environmental and socio-economic impacts of replacing the traditional feed basket with improved forages. Availability of forage seed and extension services are key enabling factors.

## Methods in brief

- **Sites.** Mid-altitude mixed farming systems with dairy potential and forage scarcity, in eight woredas of Amhara and SNNP/Sidama.
- **Design.** tricot packages of three separate options, drawn from 21 grasses, legumes and grass-legume mixtures. Woreda development agents were trained. Package labels are dated 2022.
- **Evaluation.** Best-worst questions for 19 traits plus overall preference. The results show up to 17 traits per harvest, at the first and second harvest.
- **Analysis.** Probability of outperforming Desho for biomass, by region. Kendall correlation of each trait with overall preference, by region and pooled. Plackett-Luce log-worth by trait on the pooled data.

## Stated limitations

- Familiar, well-known varieties influence farmers' preferences (listed under lessons and next steps).
- Biophysical and socio-economic factors affect both performance and preference (listed under lessons and next steps).
