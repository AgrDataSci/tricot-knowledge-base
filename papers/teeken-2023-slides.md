## Questions this paper answers

- How was tricot set up for cassava in Nigeria, and how were participants sampled?
- How does tricot extend gender-responsive mother-baby trials?
- Why do women processors matter for cassava variety choice?
- How can social covariates enter routine breeding analysis?

## Summary

These slides from a gender conference in New Delhi present IITA's move toward demand-driven, socially and gender-inclusive cassava breeding in Nigeria. They trace a sequence of research that shaped the current breeding product profile for processed cassava foods (gari and fufu), from the IITA Cassava Monitoring Study on variety adoption covering 2,500 households, through gender-responsive mother-baby trials with farmers and processors, to tricot citizen-science trials that scale the baby-trial approach for greater representativeness.

The tricot trials ran in the 2019–2020 and 2021–2022 seasons with NRCRI and IITA. Four states were chosen for their activity in cassava cultivation and processing. In each state, the two most active of three senatorial zones were selected, four communities per zone were identified with the agricultural development programme, and ten farmers per community were sampled purposively for expertise, gender and social group. This gave 80 trials per state and 320 trials in total, each with small plots of five by six plants, testing 30 varieties, a mix of popular farmer varieties and new ones.

Each participant ranked their three varieties from best to worst, overall and for specific traits, at several time points from planting to processing, with no rating scales. Varieties were assigned by sequentially balanced randomization, with ClimMob and ODK Collect for registration and data collection. Results gave a final ranking of varieties overall and per trait, with social covariates showing how preferences differ among user groups.

The central message is that women largely dominate cassava processing and marketing and therefore determine which varieties suit the whole value chain. The slides argue for integrating tricot sustainably in breeding as improved on-farm testing for variety release, standardizing automated social covariate analysis within breeding data, and including crop users as stakeholders in the breeding process.

The slides place tricot within a longer research sequence that informed IITA's cassava product profile for gari and fufu. Earlier work included a state-representative survey with participatory processing and consumer testing of preferred characteristics, gender-responsive mother-baby trials with farmers and processors, a 1000minds study of the economic value of agronomic and food quality traits, and demand creation trials. Tricot was introduced to upscale the baby-trial approach, reaching more participants and greater representativeness while keeping processors and consumers involved. The design shows how tricot was adapted for a clonal crop processed into food, with evaluation continuing from planting through processing, and with participants chosen to represent the social and task groups that shape cassava value chains.

## Methods in brief

- **Sampling.** 4 states, 2 zones per state, 4 communities per zone, 10 farmers per community, purposive by expertise, gender and social group.
- **Design.** 3 of 30 varieties per participant, sequentially balanced randomization, plots of 5 × 6 plants.
- **Evaluation.** Best-to-worst ranking overall and by trait from planting to processing.
- **Tools.** ClimMob and ODK Collect.

## Stated limitations

- Slides summarize results without full statistics. Use the peer-reviewed papers for evidence.
- Purposive sampling of experienced farmers may not represent all cassava users.
