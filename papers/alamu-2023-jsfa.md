## Questions this paper answers

- Which measurable quality traits of gari and eba explain consumer preference?
- Can instrumental texture measures replace trained sensory panels?
- How heritable are eba color and texture traits?
- Can near-infrared spectroscopy screen these traits at high throughput?

## Summary

The study defines the critical quality traits of gari and eba, measures their heritability, identifies instrumental methods breeders can use at medium to high throughput, and links the traits to consumer preferences. New cassava genotypes are adopted only if they meet end-user food quality, so breeders need measurable proxies for what consumers want.

Eighty cassava genotypes in three sets, grown at IITA sites in Nigeria in 2019–2021 and harvested at 12 months, were processed into gari and eba following RTBfoods standard operating procedures. The team measured color (L*, a*, b*), instrumental texture (hardness, adhesiveness, cohesiveness, gumminess, chewiness, springiness) and, for 13 selected genotypes, sensory texture scored by 16 trained panelists on a 0 to 10 scale. A set of ten genotypes (advanced lines, released, regional and processor-chosen local varieties) went to tricot consumer testing with 600 consumers from rural and urban areas in Osun and Benue states.

Instrumental hardness correlated with sensory hardness, and instrumental adhesiveness negatively with sensory moldability, so instrumental texture can reliably estimate sensory profiles. The first two principal components explained 85.3% of the variation in eba texture. Consumers strongly preferred eba from gari with higher lightness (L*) and redness (a*) but lower yellowness (b*), and eba with higher cohesiveness. Color traits were moderately heritable (above 30%), while most texture traits had low heritability (below 10%). NIRS calibrations for eba color were good for L* (R² 0.80) and fair for a* (0.66) but weak for b* (0.35).

Plackett-Luce trees split consumers by ethnic group, with Idoma, Igede and Yoruba participants in one node, and city consumers converging on the breeders' check TMS6. The authors conclude that gari and eba color, together with instrumental hardness and cohesiveness, are key quantitative discriminants of cassava genotypes for breeding.

NIRS also predicted several texture traits reasonably, with R² in prediction of 0.59 for chewiness, 0.54 for hardness and 0.40 for adhesiveness. The authors argue such models could already serve for screening, because the only alternatives for gari and eba texture are instrumental methods that are not cost-effective at breeding scale, but the calibrations need more genotypes with wider variation in texture to improve. Taken together, the study gives breeders a chain of evidence from consumer rankings to measurable traits to faster screening tools, which is what demand-led breeding needs to select for food quality early. The work was carried out within the RTBfoods project, whose standard operating procedures make the measurements comparable across crops and laboratories.

## Methods in brief

- **Materials.** 80 genotypes in three experiments, IITA sites in Nigeria, 2019–2020 and 2020–2021.
- **Processing.** Gari and eba following RTBfoods SOPs.
- **Measurements.** Colorimetry, instrumental texture profile analysis, sensory texture profile analysis by 16 trained panelists, NIRS calibration.
- **Genetics.** BLUEs and broad-sense heritability estimates.
- **Consumers.** Tricot test with 600 consumers on ten genotypes, Plackett-Luce trees with social covariates.

## Stated limitations

- Cohesiveness, a key preference driver, had low NIRS prediction accuracy in validation.
- Low heritability of most texture traits limits direct selection gains.
- Consumer testing covered two Nigerian states.
