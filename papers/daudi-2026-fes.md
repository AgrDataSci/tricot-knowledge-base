## Questions this paper answers

- How can station multi-environment trials and tricot on-farm trials be combined for variety release?
- Which groundnut genotypes are high-yielding and stable in Tanzania?
- Do women and men groundnut farmers prefer different traits?
- Which traits should enter the groundnut target product profile?

## Summary

Groundnut breeding must deliver high kernel yield and the traits farmers and markets want, but yield and selection responses depend on genotype-by-environment interaction and management. The study assesses genotype-by-environment interaction and farmers' trait preferences together, to select genotypes for release and to guide breeding for broad or specific adaptation while integrating gender.

Sixteen genotypes, including two commercial checks, were evaluated in a randomized complete block design across 18 environments representing groundnut agroecological zones in Tanzania. In parallel, decentralized on-farm tricot trials ran across 42 locations, with 154, 178 and 871 farmers in 2021, 2022 and 2023 respectively, in 36 villages of 10 regions.

Genotype, environment and their interaction all significantly affected kernel yield. ICGV-SM 05534 gave the highest mean kernel yield, about 927 kg/ha. A GGE biplot identified ICGV-SM 16645 and ICGV-SM 10014 as the most stable genotypes, with mean yields of about 936 and 878 kg/ha.

The tricot analysis revealed gender differences in preferences. Women valued early maturity, ease of harvesting and ease of shelling most, while men valued haulm yield. Across farmers, tan seed color and small to medium kernel size drove overall preference, and early maturity matters for the market segment.

The authors conclude that farmers' trait preferences should shape target product profiles at the design stage to increase adoption, and that the selected genotypes, test environments and preferred traits can guide breeding pipelines, targeted variety release and production for Tanzania's target populations of environments.

The GGE analysis grouped the test environments into four mega-environments, which the authors suggest can guide targeted testing and release. The convergence of women and men on marketability, small to medium kernels and tan color shows that breeders must prioritize market-preferred phenotypes, yet kernel color and seed size are not consistently treated as priority traits in groundnut breeding. The authors recommend building them into target product profiles from the start, as CIMMYT's Eastern and Southern Africa groundnut programme has begun to do. At the same time, the divergence between women and men on labor-saving traits and oil content shows the value of tailoring pipelines to gender-differentiated priorities, which they expect to raise adoption and varietal turnover. They also note that aggregated on-farm rankings from many farmers are an established way to distinguish technology performance, citing earlier tricot validation work. The study thus combines conventional stability analysis with farmer evaluation to support release decisions, an approach the authors present as vital for breeding pipelines and production in Tanzania's different target environments.

## Methods in brief

- **Station trials.** 16 genotypes (2 checks), randomized complete block design, 18 environments.
- **Analysis.** Combined ANOVA and GGE biplot for stability and mega-environments.
- **On-farm trials.** Tricot in 42 locations, 36 villages, 10 regions, 2021–2023, with sex-disaggregated rankings.
- **Preference analysis.** Plackett-Luce models of trait and overall rankings by gender.

## Stated limitations

- Yields are low in absolute terms, reflecting low-input conditions.
- On-farm trial numbers varied widely between years.
