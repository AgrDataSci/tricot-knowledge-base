## Questions this paper answers

- Why do tricot implementers often skip feedback to farmers?
- Where in the tricot process does the feedback gap start?
- What kinds of feedback materials do farmers and implementers want?
- What changes to ClimMob could make feedback routine?

## Summary

The deck reviews why farmer feedback, an integral part of tricot, is not delivered systematically. The problem statement is that the existing feedback form was not used by implementers, so participants were not treated as equal partners, undermining tricot's promise as a participatory approach.

The team followed a design process of scoping, exploring (desk research, design and heuristic review, interviews with implementers), and creating (co-design sessions and prototypes). Mapping the implementer journey showed the gap starts well before feedback sessions. Implementers are often unaware that feedback is part of the method, farmers do not understand their role or ask for feedback, sessions are not budgeted, other trial duties take priority, there is no guidance on running sessions, and the existing forms are hard to extract from ClimMob and do not feel like a fair return for farmers' effort.

Proposed solutions follow the same journey. They include communicating feedback as a core responsibility with success stories, training and recognizing field agents, a budget planner in ClimMob, information materials explaining citizen science to farmers, photos and videos to personalize feedback, reminders to implementers, a single easy feedback sheet in local languages generated by ClimMob, a workshop manual, and storytelling or gamified session formats. The team ranked ideas by impact and effort.

First prototypes were a farmer feedback sheet and a separate participation diploma. Feedback sessions highlighted local equipment constraints (WhatsApp distribution, black-and-white printing, laminated sheets), the value of recognizing farmers' contribution, managing expectations about seed availability, and the need for crop-specific, localized icons. Implementers preferred simple letter codes over complicated variety numbers.

The review places the problem in tricot's participatory promise. Without systematic feedback, farmers contribute data but receive little back, which weakens the equitable partnership that justifies the approach. The ideas therefore target implementers as much as farmers, treating feedback as a budgeted, planned step rather than an optional extra at the end of the trial. Co-design with implementers also tested practical details, such as whether sheets should show farmers how much data they contributed, whether to add management advice or information on where to obtain seed, and how to make results understandable without long explanations. These insights fed the prototypes later validated in the field (muller-2025-ux).

The review sets the stage for treating farmer feedback as a designed product with its own requirements, users and evaluation, rather than an administrative report. It also names concrete changes that ClimMob could make to lower the workload of implementers.

## Methods in brief

- **Explore.** Desk research, design review, heuristic review, interviews with tricot implementers.
- **Create.** Ideation along the implementer journey, impact-effort prioritization, co-design sessions with colleagues and implementers.
- **Prototype.** Farmer feedback sheet and participation diploma variants.

## Stated limitations

- Early-stage design work based on implementer input, before field testing with farmers.
