## Questions this paper answers

- Why do tricot implementers often skip feedback to farmers?
- Where in the tricot process does the feedback gap start?
- What feedback materials did designers and implementers propose for farmers?
- What changes to ClimMob could make feedback routine?

## Summary

The deck reviews why farmer feedback, an integral part of tricot, is not delivered systematically. The problem statement is that the existing feedback form was not used systematically by tricot trial implementers, so participants were not treated as equal partners, undermining tricot's promise as a participatory approach.

The team followed a design process of scoping, exploring (desk research, design and heuristic review, interviews with implementers), and creating (co-design sessions and prototypes). Mapping the implementer journey showed the gap starts well before feedback sessions. Implementers are often unaware that feedback is part of the method, farmers do not understand their role or ask for feedback, sessions are not budgeted, other trial duties take priority, there is no guidance on running sessions, and the existing forms are seen as not useful, hard to extract and hard to understand, and do not feel like a worthy return for farmers' participation.

Proposed solutions follow the same journey. They include communicating feedback as a core responsibility with success stories, training and recognizing field agents, a budget planner in ClimMob, information materials explaining citizen science to farmers, videos of farmers' experience and photos to personalize the feedback sheet, reminders to implementers, a single easy feedback sheet generated in ClimMob, individual reports in local languages or without text, a workshop manual, storytelling or gamified session formats, and new ranking graphics. The team placed the ideas on an impact-effort matrix and voted for the first ones to implement.

The first prototypes were three feedback sheet concepts, shown on slides branded BOLDER. A formal Diploma used medals, an informal Blackboard used podium graphics, and a black-and-white Copy could be filled in by hand. Each was rated on eight pros-and-cons criteria, including ClimMob data extraction, printing cost and meeting time. An internal feedback session with the Inclusive Design Lever team raised questions about local equipment (sharing by WhatsApp, black-and-white printing, laminated sheets). It proposed separating a participation certificate from an informative feedback sheet, and stressed crop-specific icons and managing expectations about seed availability. The refined Prototypes 1.1 were therefore a separate diploma and feedback sheet. In a co-design session, implementers appreciated the diploma, preferred abstract leaf icons, and asked for easy variety names or letters instead of complicated numbers. They said farmers want a clear local recommendation of what to plant.

The review places the problem in tricot's participatory promise. Without systematic feedback, farmers contribute data but receive little back, which weakens the equitable partnership that justifies the approach. The ideas therefore target implementers as much as farmers, treating feedback as a budgeted, planned step rather than an optional extra at the end of the trial. The internal design review also asked whether sheets should show farmers how much data they contributed, and whether to add management advice or information on where to obtain seed.

The review treats the feedback sheet as a product to design for its users, generated in ClimMob on its own rather than as a subreport of the trial report.

## Methods in brief

- **Scope.** Roles and responsibilities, a shared goal, a review of prior insights, and a problem statement.
- **Explore.** Desk research, design review, heuristic review, interviews with tricot implementers.
- **Create.** Ideation along the implementer journey, impact-effort prioritization with voting, feedback and co-design sessions with colleagues and implementers.
- **Prototype.** Three feedback sheet concepts (Diploma, Blackboard, Copy) rated on eight pros-and-cons criteria, then Prototypes 1.1, a separate participation diploma and feedback sheet, discussed with implementers.
- **Sample size.** Not reported. The deck gives no number of interviews or session participants.

## Stated limitations

- The deck states no limitations and gives no sample sizes. Farmers were not consulted directly. Getting feedback from lead farmers is listed as a possible next step (slide 20).
