## Questions this paper answers

- Which groundnut product types should breeding in Tanzania prioritise?
- How important are early maturity, fodder and oil content to groundnut farmers?
- Do Tanzanian farmers have access to groundnut seed, and would they buy it?
- What role do grain traders play in groundnut seed supply?
- How can market intelligence feed a product design team?

## Summary

The report answers research questions raised at the Tanzania groundnut product design team meeting at Naliendele in November 2022. Product design teams are meant to represent the whole value chain, from breeders and seed producers to traders, processors and farmers, and to define market segments and target product profiles. Market intelligence was asked to guide that discussion with evidence on five breeding questions and five seed system questions. The report is a descriptive slide deck with no formal tests.

It combines four sources. A trader survey covered 300 traders (87 large off-takers and 213 small traders) in 12 regions in May and June 2023. A qualitative study held 20 key informant interviews and 10 focus groups in Dodoma and Shinyanga in October and November 2022. A farmer survey of 1,100 groundnut farmers in Dodoma and Shinyanga in June 2023 included concept testing, but the report shows no concept test results. On-farm tricot testing with 1,203 farmers ran countrywide from 2021 to 2023 under the AVISA and 1000FARMS projects.

The surveyed traders sold 45,207 tons, 68% of it tan. The report groups demand into three segments. Small and medium tan (over 45% of volume, mainly Dodoma) is used for peanut butter and cooking. Large tan (over 20%, mainly Songwe) and large red (under 20%) are used for snacks, confectionery and cooking. Traders most often named taste (69%), oil content (48%), size (37%) and colour (29%) as preference drivers. Size dominates for large groundnuts and oil content for small and medium ones. In the tricot trials, overall preference correlated most strongly with marketability, grain colour and grain size, for both men and women.

Early maturity (90 to 105 days) is preferred by 74% of surveyed farmers in these arid and semi-arid areas. Later, larger types may suit highland areas. Most farmers (72%) leave crop residue on the field as mulch and only 17% feed it to livestock, so the authors see no case for breeding for fodder. There is no demand for a specialised oil groundnut. Oil content is a product profile priority for the small and medium tan segment, for peanut butter, but not for the large tan and large red segments, where size and taste come first. Half of the farmers use no inputs after planting, so the authors recommend testing new varieties under low-input conditions.

For seed systems, access is low. Among 485 farmers asked about seed sources, 61% get seed from neighbours, 31% from local markets and only 7% from out-growers or agro-dealers. Of all 1,100 farmers, 82% recycle seed all the time or most seasons. Yet 92% of the 485 would buy fresh seed if it were available, and 42% every season. Almost half of the traders said their customers plant the grain they buy, and 24% prepare grain to sell as seed, so traders are a possible seed channel. Women do most groundnut field work, know more about varieties and use groundnut as a cash reserve, so they are a key customer group.

## Methods in brief

- **Trader survey (Study 1).** Structured face-to-face questionnaire with 300 traders (87 large off-takers, 213 small or marketplace traders) in 12 regions, 17 May to 9 June 2023. The slides credit these data to the Accelerate project report.
- **Stakeholder interviews (Study 2).** 20 key informant interviews (traders, seed producers, processors, government officials) and 10 focus groups (5 with men, 5 with women, 8 to 10 people each) in Dodoma and Shinyanga, October and November 2022.
- **Farmer survey (Study 3).** Structured questionnaire with 1,100 farmers (551 men, 549 women) in Dodoma and Shinyanga, June 2023, including concept testing. Seed source and purchase questions use a subsample of 485.
- **On-farm tricot testing (Study 4).** 1,203 farmers (549 men, 654 women) countrywide, 2021 to 2023. Kendall correlations of each trait ranking with overall preference, by gender.
- **Analysis.** Descriptive percentages and charts, with no statistical tests.

## Stated limitations

The report states no limitations. It is a descriptive slide deck with no statistical tests, and the seed questions use a subsample of 485 farmers.

## Notes

- The tricot data behind the on-farm results are analysed in daudi-2026-fes.
- The concept test data are released as the replication data of this report in AgrDataSci/tricot-data. That version holds more variables than the CIMMYT Dataverse deposit.
- Market Intelligence Brief 14 cites this report with Biswanath Das among the authors. The report itself does not list him.
- The on-farm slide (p. 28) credits its results to Happy Daudi, Kauê de Sousa et al. (2023) and the AVISA and 1000FARMS projects. The trader-survey slides (pp. 20 to 27) give "Accelerate Report" as their source.
- Varieties grown are fragmented. Tilla (21%) and Lusia (16%) lead among about 30 named varieties (p. 39).
- Source issue: on the size slide (p. 22) the small-groundnut box is mislabelled "Medium". Its tonnages are those of small tan and small red.
- Source issue: the claim that confectionery use drives small and medium groundnuts holds for red types only. For tan types, large tan has the highest share (26%) (p. 27).
- Source issue: "half of them would buy every season" (p. 9) is 42% of all 485 respondents on p. 36.
- Source issue: the 15% "recycle own seed" figure on p. 9 seems to be the share of produce kept as seed (p. 47). On p. 33 all farmers say they recycle seed.
- Source issue: the executive summary says the product-type drivers were confirmed in on-farm testing (p. 8), but the tricot slide (p. 28) does not split results by product type, so it cannot by itself confirm the small/medium versus large grouping.
- Source issue: p. 8 says oil content is mentioned only for small and medium groundnuts, but 23% to 25% of traders of large types also cite it (p. 27), though much less than for small tan (78%).
- Source issue: seed source and purchase-interest results use a subsample of 485 farmers. The report does not say how it was selected (pp. 34 to 36).
- Source issue: the trader seed figures (almost half, 24%) appear only in the executive summary (p. 9), with no data slide.
