## Questions this paper answers

- Which groundnut product types should breeding in Tanzania prioritise?
- How important are early maturity, fodder and oil content to groundnut farmers?
- Do Tanzanian farmers have access to groundnut seed, and would they buy it?
- What role do grain traders play in groundnut seed supply?
- How can market intelligence feed a product design team?

## Summary

The report answers questions raised by the Tanzania groundnut product design team at Naliendele in November 2022, where breeders, seed producers, traders, processors and farmers met to define market segments and target product profiles. Market intelligence was asked to guide that discussion with evidence on breeding and seed system questions.

It combines four sources. A trader survey covered 300 traders in 12 regions in May and June 2023. A qualitative study held 20 key informant interviews and 10 focus groups in Dodoma and Shinyanga in late 2022. A farmer survey of 1,100 groundnut farmers in Dodoma and Shinyanga in June 2023 included video-based product concept testing. On-farm tricot testing with 1,203 farmers ran across the country from 2021 to 2023.

For breeding, early maturity is the dominant preference in arid and semi-arid areas. Small and medium groundnuts form one product group and large groundnuts another, with different drivers. Size matters most for large groundnuts, while oil content and confectionery use matter for small and medium ones. Colour follows regional preferences, and tan holds a larger market share than red overall. More than 70% of farmers use crop residue for mulching rather than fodder, so fodder is not a breeding target. There is no demand for a specialised oil groundnut, though oil content matters for peanut butter. Taste dominates farmers' preferences.

For seed systems, nine in ten farmers would buy groundnut seed if available, and half of them every season. Almost half of the traders said their customers plant the grain they buy, and about a quarter prepare grain to sell as seed, so traders are a channel for seed. Women emerge as a key customer group.
