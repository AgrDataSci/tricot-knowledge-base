## Questions this paper answers

- What is the current, complete guidance on running a tricot trial?
- How many participants, replicates and how much seed does a trial need?
- How should participants be sampled to represent social and task groups?
- How do I cost a tricot trial and set it up in ClimMob?
- Which metadata and SOPs should accompany a trial?
- Which statistical models apply to tricot data?
- What have African implementers learned from using tricot?

## Summary

This book is the most complete and current guide to tricot, written by the team behind the method with partners from CGIAR and national programmes, under the 1000FARMS and BOLD projects. It presents tricot, conceived in 2011, as a citizen science approach for testing technology options in their use environments, now used for on-farm variety evaluation, breeding product validation, consumer testing and related applications.

The introduction sets out what makes tricot distinctive. Small incomplete blocks of three keep the barrier to participation low regardless of farm size and reduce resources and training. A streamlined digital process from design to analysis cuts errors and costs and returns feedback quickly. Breeders gain real-time market intelligence and can separate preferences of different user groups, provided participants are characterized and chosen with social impact in mind.

The book follows the ten tricot steps (preparation, design, recruitment, distribution, execution, observation, compilation, analysis, feedback and evaluation), reusing material from the 2020 guide where it still applies and adding new chapters. Preparation covers objective setting, budgeting with standardized costing templates for grain and for root, tuber and banana crops, and onboarding to ClimMob. Design covers product management with target product profiles, sampling by social and task groups (purposive recruitment with community leaders, replacing drop-outs with similar people, linking RHoMIS data), inclusion and ethics including access-and-benefit-sharing rules for traditional varieties, trial size, plot size, SOPs and their implementation in ClimMob, and a structured metadata scheme for recruitment, consent, training, follow-up, inputs, data collection and communication of results.

Trial-size guidance is concrete. Aim for at least 30 replicates per option, since rank-based estimates are unreliable when replication is too low, and at least 10 replicates per location and 10 to 20 per geographic area to detect location-specific effects. For about 12 entries, 100 to 200 farms give solid results, and a first project should start with 100 to 200 farmers. Interactive calculators estimate participants and seed. For example, 12 options with 30 replicates need 120 participants and 360 plots, and 0.2 kg per plot gives 72 kg of seed, or 79.2 kg with a 10% buffer. Small plots are favored, as in a Ghana sweetpotato tricot that used 6 m² plots with 20 vines against the 30 m² and 100 vines recommended on station. Each field agent can usually handle 20 to 25 farmers.

Later chapters cover data collection with ODK and ClimMob, statistical models (Plackett-Luce for rankings, plus models for numeric traits and genetic parameters), the redesigned farmer feedback process, evaluation of each cycle, curated resources, and case studies in which African implementers describe practical lessons, such as multiplying planting material early and the difficulty of seeing which trade-offs farmers make when ranking.

## Methods in brief

- **Structure.** Ten steps of a tricot cycle, each with one or more chapters by specialist authors.
- **Tools.** Sample-size calculator (three products per subject, alpha and beta of 0.05), seed calculator, sampling tool with travel-time estimates, costing templates, ClimMob SOP templates.
- **Standards.** SOPs and metadata vocabularies for trial documentation.
- **Analysis.** Plackett-Luce models and extensions for covariates, numeric traits and genetic parameters.

## Stated limitations

- Rankings alone do not reveal which trade-offs farmers make, which case-study implementers raised.
- Small plots do not suit every crop or trait, and some traits need repeated harvests or special protocols.
- Several chapters reuse 2020 guide material and may be updated in later versions of the book.
