## Questions this paper answers

- What do Tanzanian farmers want from new sorghum seed products?
- How can video-based product concept testing inform sorghum market segments?
- Should breeding invest in hybrids, home use, malting or fodder sorghum?

## Summary

The brief applies video-based product concept testing (VPCT) to sorghum in Tanzania, one of the largest sorghum producers in Eastern Africa, to inform CGIAR and national discussions on market segmentation and target product profiles.

Through engagements with breeders, farmers and industry, the team developed seven seed product concepts. Five target end use (home use, feed and food, industrial malting, food and fodder, forage), one targets the cropping system (intercropping) and one targets the material type (hybrid). The team sampled 1,100 farmers in the Dodoma and Shinyanga regions, and each farmer viewed and ranked three concepts.

Farmers were most likely to choose the hybrid concept as their most preferred, followed by home use. On this basis, the brief proposes revisions to the current set of sorghum market segments and target product profiles for Tanzania, and gives the existing segment sizes and the share of breeding investment allocated to each.

The full ranking ran hybrid, home use, intercropping, feed and food, food and fodder, industrial malting and forage. The authors link the preference for hybrids to growing interest in sorghum as a cash crop for brewing and export, noting that Tanzania's regional sorghum exports grew by 300% over five years. The strong ranking of home use is notable because the concept was described as yielding 20% less, which shows that sorghum's value in rural diets can outweigh yield. The brief calls for further work with farmers, processors and food scientists to define and measure what makes sorghum taste good. Across all concepts, 70% of farmers preferred white grain, a must-have for national and regional markets that is also associated with better-tasting foods.

For segmentation, the brief recommends prioritizing the hybrid segment, considering new segments for home use, intercropping, food and fodder, and food and feed, and keeping forage as a future segment. It sets these proposals against the current segments, which cover 561,989, 312,216 and 8,742 hectares and receive 60%, 20% and 20% of the 2023–2024 breeding investment. Each concept was presented through a scripted video describing its benefits and trade-offs, and the scripts are reproduced in an annex.

The video scripts show how trade-offs were made explicit. The feed and food concept promised grain with improved protein for chickens and pigs but warned that cooking qualities received less attention. The home use concept prioritized cooking quality, with higher iron and zinc, good flour yield and a sweet taste in uji or ugali.

## Methods in brief

- **Concepts.** Seven sorghum concepts developed with breeders, farmers and industry.
- **Design.** Each farmer ranked three concepts presented by video.
- **Sample.** 1,100 farmers in Dodoma and Shinyanga.

## Stated limitations

- A brief rather than a full research paper, with limited statistical detail.
- Results cover two regions of Tanzania.
