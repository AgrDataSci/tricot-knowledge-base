## Questions this paper answers

- Which eba quality traits drive consumer acceptance in Nigeria and Cameroon?
- Are cassava food preferences homogeneous across countries, cultures and urban or rural settings?
- How can tricot consumer data be linked with laboratory measurements?
- Which cassava genotypes are acceptable for eba in both countries?

## Summary

The study informs cassava breeders about the eba quality traits consumers value. Eba (gari-fufu in Cameroon) is made by mixing gari, a toasted cassava semolina, with hot water. Nigeria and Cameroon are multi-ethnic, with diverse processing methods and preferences, so the authors test how stable acceptability is across cultural and environmental settings.

Cassava genotypes (new improved clones, local varieties chosen by champion processors, and breeders' checks) were grown at IITA stations in Nigeria and an IRAD station in Cameroon, processed into gari, and prepared as eba. Consumers in villages, towns and cities tasted sequentially balanced, randomized sets of three eba samples and ranked them following tricot. In the Littoral zone of Cameroon, 400 consumers took part (80 in villages, 120 in towns, 200 in the city). Data from both countries were combined and linked to laboratory analyses of gari and eba color and texture. The authors note that testing more than four or five samples per consumer with a Likert scale causes fatigue, a problem tricot avoids.

Consumers in both countries preferred eba with higher cohesiveness and eba from brighter gari, with Cameroonian consumers especially preferring lower redness and yellowness. Nigerian consumers preferred harder, springier and chewier eba, while Cameroonian consumers preferred lower values. Trends for gari solubility and swelling power also differed. Stickiness to the fingers was disliked.

The older improved variety TMS30572 was a benchmark with superior eba across regions of Nigeria, while the recent release Game Changer did very well in Cameroon, losing slightly only on stretchability. The new releases Obasanjo-2 and TM14F1278P0003 showed good stability and acceptability in both countries, and color and smoothness explained weaker performance of some clones against checks.

The authors conclude that eba preferences are surprisingly homogeneous, which supports breeding varieties with wide adaptation, and stress that clear differences from existing varieties are needed to motivate adoption.

The authors argue that the wide acceptance of a single genotype across diverse settings in Nigeria, and of three new improved varieties in both countries, means breeders can aim for varieties with wider acceptability and larger target areas than cultural diversity might suggest. They frame the work as studying genotype-by-environment-by-society interactions, and recommend combining consumer rankings with laboratory measurements so that breeders can screen for the textures and colors consumers value. The study also illustrates how tricot consumer testing scales across countries while keeping each taster's task manageable. It complements the Nigerian study by Alamu et al. 2023.

## Methods in brief

- **Materials.** Improved clones, processor-selected local varieties and breeders' checks grown at stations in Nigeria and Cameroon.
- **Processing.** Gari produced, then eba prepared in a standard way.
- **Consumer test.** Tricot with sequentially balanced randomized sets of three samples, overall acceptability and attributes ranked.
- **Laboratory.** Color (L*, a*, b*), texture by double compression (texturometer), solubility and swelling power.
- **Analysis.** Plackett-Luce models, model-based recursive partitioning on country and social covariates, Kendall's tau, reliability against checks.

## Stated limitations

- Some social covariates were significant only in the Nigerian data, so pooled analyses can hide local differences.
- The set of genotypes differed partly between countries.
