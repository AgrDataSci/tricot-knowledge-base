- Can farmers answer in their own language, by voice, during a tricot or ClimMob data collection?
- Does Sikia work without an internet connection?
- Which languages does Sikia support now, and which are coming?

- How does Sikia handle consent and data protection?

## Summary

Laurens Goormachtigh, Stephen Mutuvi and Lennart Woltering present Sikia in this two-page flyer from the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT. Sikia means "to listen" in Swahili. It is an AI tool that lets research teams collect open-ended spoken answers from many people in their own languages and turn them into structured data. It is integrated with ClimMob, so ClimMob projects can collect voice answers alongside the usual rankings and questions.

The flyer frames the problem as a trade-off between depth and scale. In-depth interviews are slow and costly, and in under-resourced languages, translation and manual coding add bias and lose local nuance. Sikia targets people who speak only local languages and live where connectivity is poor.

It works in four steps. Teams design the interview with an AI form builder, which accepts ODK .xls forms and ClimMob. An interviewer holds an open conversation while Sikia listens on an ordinary Android phone or tablet. Sikia tracks what is still missing and suggests the next question in real time, on the device, without connectivity. When the phone syncs, speech becomes transcripts, and language models trained on local languages turn them into themes, coded answers and a written report.

Teams get survey data with transcripts and English translations as columns (Excel, CSV, JSON, SPSS, Stata), full transcripts, coded themes and sentiment, a PDF report with findings, quotes, limitations and methods, and the audio recordings.

Swahili, Amharic and Hausa are live. English, Spanish, French, Arabic and other high-resource languages work off the shelf. Oromo is being refined, and Chichewa, Yoruba and Shona are committed for 2027. The team has used Sikia for product concept testing in Ethiopia and Tanzania and to evaluate rice research in Colombia, and plans Hausa consumer surveys in Nigeria in early 2027.

All access goes through an authenticated service. Data are encrypted in transit and at rest, recordings are shared only through expiring links, personal information is scrambled before use, and each study carries its consent text in every language.

## Methods in brief

- **Design.** AI form builder that accepts ODK .xls forms and ClimMob questionnaires.
- **Collection.** Open conversation recorded on an ordinary Android phone or tablet, in the respondent's language.
- **Probing.** Follow-up questions suggested on the device, adapted to each answer, with no connectivity needed.
- **Processing.** After sync, speech models built for low-resource African languages transcribe and translate, and language models code answers into themes and write a report.
- **Ethics.** Authenticated access, encryption, expiring links for media, scrambled personal data, consent text per language, read aloud or tapped.

## Stated limitations

- Oromo support is still being refined, and Chichewa, Yoruba and Shona are not available before 2027.
- The flyer is promotional and reports no accuracy, cost or data-quality figures.
