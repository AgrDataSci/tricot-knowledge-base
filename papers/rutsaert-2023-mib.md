## Questions this paper answers

- What is video-based product concept testing (VPCT) and why use it?
- How can future market segments be identified before breeding starts?
- Which maize concepts should inform future CGIAR market segmentation in East Africa?

## Summary

CGIAR's market segmentation for maize in East Africa had four segments based on agro-ecological zone and maturity class. Because developing a variety takes many years, the brief asks which future segments should already shape breeding investments, and introduces video-based product concept testing (VPCT) as a tool to answer that question with farmers.

The brief explains the conceptual and methodological basis of VPCT, which combines concepts from product innovation with the triadic comparisons of tricot. Seven new product concepts were developed with breeders, seed companies and farmers (home use, intercropping, drought avoidance, nutritious, feed with yellow maize, green maize, and food and fodder). They were tested alongside the resilient benchmark concept, the current breeding target. Each farmer watched videos of three concepts and ranked them, through household surveys with 1,200 farmers in each of Kenya and Uganda, 2,400 in total, in wet mid-altitude areas.

Drought avoidance ranked high in Uganda, while intercropping stood out in Kenya. Maturity preferences also differed sharply between the countries. The brief also flags emerging opportunities from changes in context, such as nutritious maize, yellow maize for feed and green maize.

The authors argue that concept testing makes a strong case for new investments that integrate farmers' agronomic practices and preferences into breeding, on-farm testing and seed systems, and that it should feed into discussions on market segments and target product profiles.

The brief discusses what the results imply for current segments. Women showed strong interest in the home-use concept, built around post-harvest use and family consumption. The authors argue this may not warrant a separate segment, because the existing mid-maturity resilient segment already targets food use, but that its target product profile should be checked for how well consumption traits are specified. Concepts can also be built around production challenges outside current pipelines, such as harvesting in groundnut, to compare possible future priorities with current breeding directions.

Once future segments are identified, the authors note, programmes need estimates of the crop area each would cover and how much it would take from existing segments, considering seed production, distribution and farmers' access to information, through qualitative or geospatial approaches. They conclude that the method met its goals of being cost-efficient, quick to roll out with partners, intuitive for farmers, and able to compare current and potential future products, while still needing further development and testing. The full analysis is published in Donovan et al. 2025 (donovan-2025-expagric), with the statistical detail behind these results.

## Methods in brief

- **Concepts.** Seven new concepts plus one benchmark, developed with breeders, seed companies and farmers.
- **Design.** Each farmer viewed three of the eight concept videos and ranked them.
- **Sample.** Household surveys with 1,200 farmers per country.
