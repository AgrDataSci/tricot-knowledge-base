## Questions this paper answers

- What is ClimMob and what does it do in a tricot trial?
- How is a tricot trial designed, registered and collected in ClimMob?
- How does ClimMob link to Crop Ontology, ODK and R?
- How usable is ClimMob, and who uses it?
- Where is ClimMob development heading?

## Summary

ClimMob is the software that makes tricot workable at scale. Experimental citizen science needs a workflow that farmers can follow and that gives breeders and advisors relevant results, and it needs software that lets many organizations design trials, collect data and analyze results without specialist staff. The paper describes how ClimMob was designed, its architecture and functions, and how user feedback shaped it.

Design and workflow were developed together. The resulting workflow is tricot, where farmers compare three technologies with simple rankings. ClimMob organizes the work in three domains. In the design domain, users define technologies and their options and build a sequentially balanced incomplete block design, for example 10 varieties in packages of three for 20 farmers. In the registration and data collection domain, field agents register participants and collect data through Open Data Kit forms generated by the platform, with a dashboard tracking progress. Traits are linked to Crop Ontology, so data are standardized across projects. In the analysis domain, R packages on CRAN produce overview reports for trial managers and personalized result sheets for each participant, and climatrends supplies climate indices for Plackett-Luce trees.

The software follows component-based software engineering, built on open-source components with broad communities (ODK, ODK Tools, the PyUtilib Component Architecture) to keep it maintainable. Constant feedback and short communication lines between developers and users were key.

ClimMob had close to 800 users, mostly in the Global South, with CGIAR the largest single group but public organizations and universities the majority together. The private sector is small but important for financial sustainability. In 2022, 16 users rated ClimMob 68.1 on the System Usability Scale and 25 users gave a UMUX-Lite of 73.0, around the middle of reference software. Planned work, continued under the 1000FARMS project, includes better user experience, more data collection channels, integration with breeding platforms and genomic data, and optional household surveys such as RHoMIS.

The paper explains the early design choices. The team realized that every step of the experimental cycle needed digital support, so workflow and software had to be designed together. Farmers take a package home, plant it and observe independently, ranking options for several characteristics and preferences, with optional quantitative measurements. Data are then aggregated and analyzed by an automated workflow that returns results in about ten minutes, depending on dataset size, so feedback reaches trial managers and farmers quickly. This fast loop, together with standard trait definitions, is what allows many different organizations to run comparable trials.

## Methods in brief

- **Architecture.** Component-based software engineering on open-source components (ODK, ODK Tools, PyUtilib).
- **Design.** Sequentially balanced incomplete block designs with packages of three options.
- **Data.** ODK forms generated per project, traits linked to Crop Ontology.
- **Analysis.** R packages on CRAN (ClimMobTools, gosset, PlackettLuce, climatrends) for reports and participant feedback.
- **Usability.** System Usability Scale (16 users) and UMUX-Lite (25 users) in the first quarter of 2022.

## Stated limitations

- Usability is average, not high, so user experience needs improvement.
- Integration with breeding databases and genomic data was still incomplete.
- Financial sustainability depends partly on a small private-sector user base.
