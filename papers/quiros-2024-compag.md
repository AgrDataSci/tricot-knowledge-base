## Questions this paper answers

- What is ClimMob and what does it do in a tricot trial?
- How is a tricot trial designed, registered and collected in ClimMob?
- How does ClimMob link to Crop Ontology, ODK and R?
- How usable is ClimMob, and who uses it?
- Where is ClimMob development heading?

## Summary

ClimMob is the software built to support tricot trials at scale. Experimental citizen science needs a workflow that farmers can follow and that gives breeders and advisors relevant results. It also needs software that lets many organizations design trials, collect data and analyze results without much external help. The paper describes how ClimMob was designed, its architecture and functions, and how user feedback shaped it.

Design and workflow were developed together. The resulting workflow is tricot, where farmers compare three technologies with simple rankings. ClimMob organizes the work in three domains. The Libraries domain holds shared lists of technology options and questions. Where possible, system-wide questions are linked to ontology terms, and ranking traits are linked to Crop Ontology. The Planning + Management domain has three modules. In Trial design, users choose technology options and build a sequentially balanced, approximately A-optimal incomplete block design, for example 10 varieties in packages of three for 20 farmers. In Participant registration and Data collection, field agents use Open Data Kit forms generated by the platform and link each participant to a package by scanning its QR code. A dashboard tracks progress. In the Analysis domain, R packages on CRAN produce overview reports for trial managers and personalized result sheets for each participant, and climatrends supplies climate indices for Plackett-Luce trees.

The software follows component-based software engineering, built on open-source components with broad communities (ODK, ODK Tools, the PyUtilib Component Architecture) to keep it maintainable. Constant feedback and short communication lines between developers and users were key.

ClimMob had close to 800 users, mostly in the Global South, with CGIAR the largest single group but public organizations and universities the majority together. The private sector is small but important for financial sustainability. In 2022, 16 users rated ClimMob 68.1 on the System Usability Scale and 25 users gave a UMUX-Lite of 73.0, around the middle of reference software. Planned work covers four areas: better user experience, more data collection options (Excel-designed ODK forms through FormShare, for example to add RHoMIS household surveys, and USSD, IVR and SMS channels for remote farmers), integration with breeding platforms such as the Enterprise Breeding System and with genomic data, and reports tailored to different decision-makers. Part of this work runs under 1000FARMS, a 4-year project.

The paper explains the early design choices. Trials are blind, and ranking was chosen over rating because it needs little training and leads to fewer errors. Farmers take a package home, plant it and observe independently, ranking options for several characteristics and preferences, with optional quantitative measurements. Data are then aggregated and analyzed by an automated workflow that returns results in about ten minutes, depending on dataset size (section 3.3 says 3 to 7 minutes), so feedback reaches trial managers and farmers quickly. Version 3 added standard names for technologies and traits because trials had named traits in slightly different ways, which made it hard to combine data across seasons and trial managers.

## Methods in brief

- **History.** Version 1 (2013) was an offline R prototype without data collection. Version 2 (2015) was an online PHP platform with an Android app. Version 3 (2019), described here, is a Python platform using ODK.
- **Architecture.** Component-based software engineering on open-source components (ODK, ODK Tools, PyUtilib).
- **Design.** Incomplete blocks of three, approximately A-optimal (Kirchhoff index of the comparison graph) so the design is robust to lost plots, and sequentially balanced so consecutive package numbers give balance across villages, farm types or gender. Options are balanced across positions within packages.
- **Data.** ODK forms generated per project, participants linked to packages by QR code, triadic traits linked to Crop Ontology.
- **Analysis.** R packages on CRAN (ClimMobTools, gosset, PlackettLuce, climatrends, chirps) for reports and participant feedback.
- **Usability.** System Usability Scale (16 users) and UMUX-Lite (25 users) in the first quarter of 2022.

## Stated limitations

- The automated analysis covers one ClimMob project at a time. Combining projects needs R skills (p6).
- Data collection with ODK needs field agents with Android phones and at least periodic internet access, so farmers in very remote places cannot yet report directly. USSD, IVR and SMS channels were only planned (p9).
- ClimMob produces one general report, not reports tailored to different decision-makers such as breeding programmes and variety release committees (p9).
- Advanced users could not yet use their own Excel-designed ODK forms with skip logic (p9).
- Integration with breeding platforms such as the Enterprise Breeding System, and with genomic data, was planned but not yet in place (p9).
- Usability was close to the median for software (SUS 68.1, closest to "good"). The authors aim to maintain or improve this score (p8, p9).

## Notes

- ClimMob is a live platform. Check climmob.net and the ClimMobTools CRAN page for current features rather than relying on the 2024 description.
- ClimMob now has Sikia enabled for voice-based, multilingual data collection (goormachtigh-2026-flyer), which postdates this paper.
- Outside this paper (not in the source): ClimMob is currently developed by a consortium of the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (https://alliancebioversityciat.org/tools-innovations/climmob) and MrBot Software Solutions (https://mrbotcr.com), which is in charge of its support and maintenance. In the paper, co-author B. Madriz is affiliated with MrBot.
- Besides crop varieties, the approach had been applied to fertilizers and to food products from different varieties (p2).
- The authors offer commercial ClimMob services and keep a free version for public institutions in the Global South (p10).
- Source issue: the paper gives the analysis time as about 10 minutes in section 2 (p2) and 3 to 7 minutes in section 3.3 (p6). Both depend on dataset size.
- Source issue: Fig. 10 (approximate chart reading) shows CGIAR ~38%, public sector ~27%, universities ~22% and private sector ~13% of users. Public plus university is about half, so "majority" in the text (p8) is borderline. Section 6.4 (p9) also says most users are in public or NGO sectors, a category Fig. 10 does not show.
- Source issue: the text says each option occurs equally often as first, second or third in a package (p5). In Table 1 this holds for 8 of 10 varieties, but INTA Rojo and INTA Negro appear 3 times in one position and once in another (card's own count).
- Source issue: some citations do not match the reference list. Table 2 cites ClimMobTools as De Sousa et al. 2021a and gosset as 2022b (p8), and FormShare is cited as Quiros 2022 but listed as 2022b (p9, p12).
