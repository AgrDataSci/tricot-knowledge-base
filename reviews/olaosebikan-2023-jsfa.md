The card follows the abstract closely and gets the broad story right. But it carries the abstract's own errors into the card, and it misses what the tables show. The most important fix is the trait results: the card says consumers in both countries preferred more cohesive eba, and the gist says trait preferences are similar across cultures. Table 4 (p. 4778, PDF p. 9) shows that cohesiveness relates to liking only in Nigeria, and that several texture and gari relations reverse between the two countries. The paper's "surprisingly homogeneous" conclusion is about how some genotypes rank, not about trait drivers. The card also calls TMS3 a "new release" although Table 1 lists it as a non-released advanced clone (p. 4772, PDF p. 3). The Nigerian sample size (600) and the fact that country was the main split in the Plackett-Luce tree are missing.

## Fixes needed

1. **Trait results and gist (high).** The card says "Consumers in both countries preferred eba with higher cohesiveness and eba from brighter gari, with Cameroonian consumers especially preferring lower redness and yellowness. Nigerian consumers preferred harder, springier and chewier eba, while Cameroonian consumers preferred lower values." The gist says preferences for cohesive, bright eba are "surprisingly similar across cultures, with a few country differences". The source: cohesiveness is significant in the pooled data (Table 3, p. 4777, PDF p. 8) and in Nigeria (estimate 89.8, P = 3.3 × 10⁻¹⁷) but not in Cameroon (P = 0.93) (Table 4, p. 4778, PDF p. 9). Brightness (L*) is significant within both countries but not in the pooled data (P = 0.835). In Cameroon only redness, not yellowness, is significant (b* P = 0.83), and the text on p. 4774 (PDF p. 5) names only brightness and redness. In Cameroon only springiness and resilience relate to liking (both negative). Hardness and chewiness are not significant there. Swelling power and solubility reverse sign between the countries. Country is the main split in the Plackett-Luce tree (P = 6.7 × 10⁻⁴, Fig. 3, p. 4776, PDF p. 7). The "surprisingly homogeneous" conclusion (p. 4770) rests on genotype acceptance. Replace Summary paragraph 3 with:

   > Country was the main covariate splitting the Plackett-Luce tree of overall liking. Pooled, consumers preferred more cohesive, harder and springier eba from less yellow gari. Within each country, consumers preferred brighter gari, and Cameroonians also lower redness. Nigerian consumers preferred more cohesive, harder, springier and chewier eba that was less gummy and less adhesive. Cameroonian consumers preferred lower springiness and resilience, and other texture measures were unrelated to liking there. Nigerians preferred gari with lower swelling power and solubility, Cameroonians higher values. The authors link these contrasts to stronger fermentation in Nigeria and different ways of mixing gari with hot water. Taste correlated most with overall liking (Kendall tau 0.62), possibly inflated because eba was served without sauce.

   Replace the gist with (29 words):

   > tricot tests with 1000 eba consumers in Nigeria and Cameroon found brighter gari liked everywhere and some genotypes accepted in both countries, but texture and solubility preferences differed by country

2. **TMS3 is not a release (Contradicted).** The card says "The new releases Obasanjo-2 and TM14F1278P0003 showed good stability". Table 1 lists TMS14F1278P0003 (TMS3) as an advanced clone. The methods call TMS1, TMS2 and TMS3 "non-released advanced clones" (p. 4772, PDF p. 3). The card copies the abstract's typo in the ID ("TM14F..." instead of "TMS14F..."). Only Game Changer and Obasanjo-2 were released (2020). Replace with the paragraph 4 text in fix 4.

3. **Sample sizes and design (high omission, plus Imprecise claims).** The card gives only the 400 Cameroonian consumers. Nigeria had 300 consumer tests in each of Osun and Benue (villages 120, towns 100, cities 80), so 600 in Nigeria and 1000 in total (p. 4773, PDF p. 4, Fig. 3 nodes n = 400 and n = 600). The card says the genotypes grew at "IITA stations in Nigeria". The source names Ago-owu and Ottobi stations but does not say they are IITA stations (p. 4772). The card omits the regionally popular varieties (Cassava Monitoring Study), the 13 genotypes, and the fermentation gradient that the authors use to explain the country differences (p. 4772). The card says lab analyses covered "gari and eba color and texture", but color was measured on gari only (Table 2, p. 4773). Replace Summary paragraph 2 with:

   > Thirteen genotypes were grown at two stations in Nigeria (Osun and Benue states) and at the IRAD Njombé station in Cameroon. Five were tested everywhere: the 2020 releases Game Changer and Obasanjo-2 and the advanced clones TMS1, TMS2 and TMS3. The rest were regionally popular varieties, champion processors' local favorites and breeders' checks. Gari was fermented for 4 to 5 days in Osun, 3 in Benue and 2 in the Littoral zone. Each consumer tasted a balanced, randomized set of three eba samples and chose the best and worst, following tricot. There were 300 consumers in each Nigerian state and 400 in the Littoral zone of Cameroon (80 in villages, 120 in towns, 200 in the city). Results were linked to laboratory measures of gari color, solubility and swelling power and of eba texture. The authors note, citing a personal communication, that more than four or five samples per consumer on a Likert scale cause fatigue, which tricot avoids.

4. **Genotype results (Imprecise).** "Losing slightly only on stretchability" (Game Changer) and "color and smoothness explained weaker performance of some clones" are both too narrow. The discussion says Game Changer lost a little "on mainly stretchability" (p. 4775, PDF p. 6). The results list Game Changer's weaker traits against checks as smoothness, moldability and stretchability. Color and smoothness apply to TMS3 only, and color and stretchability to Obasanjo-2 (p. 4775 to 4776). TMS1 and TMS2 underperformed in both countries (p. 4774). Replace Summary paragraph 4 with:

   > The older improved variety TMS30572 (TMS6, released 1984) was a benchmark with superior eba across both Nigerian states. Game Changer performed especially well in Cameroon, comparable to the best local check Sape. Obasanjo-2 and the advanced clone TMS14F1278P0003 (TMS3) showed good stability and acceptability in both countries, while TMS1 and TMS2 underperformed in both. Where these three promising genotypes lost to checks, it was on color and smoothness (TMS3), smoothness, moldability and stretchability (Game Changer), and color and stretchability (Obasanjo-2).

5. **Conclusions (Imprecise, plus duplication).** "Supports breeding varieties with wide adaptation" shifts the meaning. The authors say "wider acceptability" and larger target areas (p. 4770). Agronomic adaptation was not tested. Paragraph 6 repeats paragraph 5. The fermentation-based recommendation of two lab procedures (p. 4778) is missing. Replace Summary paragraphs 5 and 6 with one paragraph:

   > The authors conclude that eba preferences are surprisingly homogeneous, which could help breeders develop varieties with wider acceptability and larger target areas. They stress that clear differences from existing varieties are needed to motivate adoption. They frame the work as genotype-by-environment-by-society interactions and recommend linking consumer rankings with laboratory measurements so breeders can screen for the textures and colors consumers value. Because fermentation explains much of the variation, they suggest two laboratory procedures, one with more and one with less fermentation. It complements the Nigerian study by Alamu et al. 2023.

   With fixes 1 and 3 to 5 and the original paragraph 1 kept, the Summary is 497 words. To keep a margin, paragraph 1 can be shortened to: "The study informs cassava breeders about the eba quality traits consumers value. Eba (gari-fufu in Cameroon) is gari, a toasted cassava semolina, mixed with hot water. Because Nigeria and Cameroon are multi-ethnic, with diverse processing and preferences, the authors test how stable acceptability is across cultural and environmental settings." (The 497 count already uses this shorter paragraph 1.)

6. **Stated limitations (Imprecise).** Neither bullet is a limitation the authors state. The first adds the card's own inference ("pooled analyses can hide local differences"). The second is a design fact. The authors do state these: the high taste correlation may come from the tasting setting and from serving eba without sauce (p. 4777, PDF p. 8), and the exact causes of the texture results need further research (p. 4777). Replace the section with:

   > - The high correlation of taste with overall liking may be inflated, because tasting draws attention to taste and eba was served without the usual sauce.
   > - The authors call for further research to establish the causes behind the texture preferences observed.

   Move the two current bullets to Notes as: "Card's observation: other social covariates were significant only when the Nigerian data were analyzed alone (p. 4774), and the consumer-ranked traits and some genotypes differed between countries (only color, taste and stretchability were common, p. 4773)."

7. **Methods in brief (Imprecise).** "Eba prepared in a standard way" mixes two procedures. For the consumer test, one local preparer per region made eba the local way, with an optimal water-to-gari ratio per variety (p. 4772 to 4773). The laboratory eba followed the RTBfoods SOP (p. 4773). The ADMM step that links lab features to Plackett-Luce worth is missing (p. 4774). Replace three bullets with:

   > - **Materials.** Thirteen genotypes in randomized complete block trials at Ago-owu (Osun) and Ottobi (Benue), Nigeria, and Njombé (IRAD), Cameroon. Two 2020 releases and three advanced clones were in all locations, plus regionally popular varieties, champion processors' local favorites and breeders' checks per location.
   > - **Processing.** Three champion processors per location made gari the traditional way, all varieties in one batch. For consumer tests, one local preparer per region made eba with an optimal water-to-gari ratio for each variety. Laboratory eba followed the RTBfoods standard procedure.
   > - **Analysis.** Plackett-Luce models, model-based recursive partitioning on country and social covariates, Plackett-Luce with genotype features (ADMM algorithm) to link lab measures to liking, Kendall's tau between traits and overall liking, and reliability against local and regional checks (gosset).
