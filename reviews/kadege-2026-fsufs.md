The card is accurate and well built. Every key number checks out against the text and tables, and two of the source's own problems are already flagged in Notes. The main gaps are elsewhere. The card does not warn that the reason counts in Tables 3 to 5 do not fit the design (youth seem to be counted twice), and it does not say that the paper gives no Plackett-Luce worth estimates or confidence intervals. It also omits the authors' own limitation that they measured perceptions rather than trait performance, and it drops men's strong market preference. The keyword list has 14 terms, above the limit of 12.

## Fixes needed

1. **Add Notes on the count inconsistencies and missing model output (high).** The card does not flag these. My calculation from Tables 3 to 5 (pages 6 to 8): with 372 farmers giving three reasons each, the maximum is 1,116 reasons, but Table 3 sums to 1,101. First reasons in the Northern zone add up to 140 (Table 4, p. 7), more than the 124 farmers in its two districts. Table 5 (p. 8) sums to 1,392 reasons. First reasons by women, men and youth add up to 169 + 205 + 93 = 467, more than 372. Trait totals also differ from Table 3 (high yield 79 + 158 + 38 = 275 in Table 5 against 236 in Table 3). Also, 53.9% of 372 is 200.5, so the participation shares cannot come from 372 farmers. Figure 1 (p. 5) shows only rank positions, with no worth estimates, confidence intervals or test of overall preference. Add these Notes bullets:

   > - The reason counts do not fit the design. With 372 farmers giving three reasons each, the maximum is 1,116, but Table 3 sums to 1,101. First reasons in the Northern zone add up to 140, more than its 124 farmers (Table 4). Table 5 sums to 1,392 reasons, and women, men and youth together give 467 first reasons, so youth are probably also counted as women or men. Trait totals in Table 5 differ from Table 3 (high yield 275 against 236). Compare groups by rank, not by raw counts. (Our calculation from Tables 3 to 5.)
   > - The 53.9% and 46.1% shares do not fit 372 farmers (53.9% of 372 is 200.5, our calculation). The paper does not give the denominator or say how youth relate to the women and men counts.
   > - The paper names the Plackett-Luce package but reports only rank positions (Figure 1). It gives no worth estimates, confidence intervals or test of overall preference between genotypes, and no ranking of genotypes for yield or disease. The abstract still calls NUA 48, NUA 64, Sweet Violet and VTT923-23-10 top performers for yield.

2. **Correct the gender sentences in the Summary (medium).** The card says men favoured "yield, disease resistance, intercropping and early maturity", which leaves out market preference. Table 5 (p. 8) ranks market preference third for men with 117 mentions, more than women's 87, and the discussion says men emphasised agronomic and market traits (p. 11). The card also gives "unsold beans" as a reason, but the source says sales could be delayed (p. 5). Replace the second and third sentences of the gender paragraph with:

   > Women put post-harvest traits higher than men did (short cooking time, no flatulence, good taste, cooking with banana), although their top three reasons were market preference, disease resistance and high yield. In interviews, women explained that aggregators collect market-preferred beans at the farm gate, while other beans and diseased plots need men's help, which can delay sales and spraying. Men favoured yield, disease resistance and market preference, then intercropping and early maturity, and youth favoured premium price, market preference and colour.

3. **Add the authors' main stated limitation (high).** Section 4.6 (p. 12) says the study focused on farmer perceptions rather than quantitative measurements of trait performance, and asks for on-farm performance evaluations. Section 4.6 (p. 13) adds consumer preferences and climate-resilience traits as future work. Add to Stated limitations:

   > - The study recorded farmer perceptions rather than quantitative measurements of trait performance, so future work should add on-farm performance evaluations to validate the traits farmers named.
   > - Future studies should link on-farm traits to consumer, market and nutrition needs and add climate-resilience traits such as drought and heat tolerance.

4. **Trim keywords to 12 (template).** The card has 14. Replace with:

   > keywords: common bean, Tanzania, tricot, trait preferences, gender, youth, anthracnose, market preference, intercropping, cooking with banana, red mottled, PVS

5. **Strengthen not_established (medium).** Districts were chosen for the highest anthracnose incidence (p. 2), and the paper shows no statistical test of overall genotype preference. Replace with:

   > not_established: that men are more involved than women in bean production nationally (participation shares come from one trial sample); effects across seasons (single season); measured yield or disease differences (yield was ranked by grain volume, disease by field-agent scores, and only ranks were analysed); that tricot data alone yield the detailed trait evaluations of PVS; that the preferences hold for all Tanzanian bean areas (six districts chosen for the highest anthracnose incidence, farmers screened by eligibility criteria); that the top genotypes differ significantly in overall preference (only rank positions are shown, without worth estimates or confidence intervals)

6. **Fix the author attribution in the Summary (low).** The card calls it "a TARI and CIAT study". The first and last authors are at NM-AIST, and the affiliations are NM-AIST, TARI and CIAT (p. 1). Replace the third sentence of the first paragraph with:

   > Edith Kadege and colleagues from NM-AIST, TARI and CIAT, funded through a tricot PhD scholarship, used tricot to capture preferences across the season and across three agro-ecological zones.

7. **Soften the PVS claim (low).** The card says PVS participants "do not represent" the diversity of environments. The source says they "might not accurately reflect" the farming population (p. 12) and that limited involvement contrasts with diverse agroecosystems (p. 2). Change "and do not represent the diversity of bean-growing environments" to:

   > and may not represent the diversity of bean-growing environments.

8. **Fix Note 1 (low).** The abstract (p. 1) does not rank criteria by zone. Only the results (Table 4, p. 7, and text on p. 5) say high yield first and disease resistance second in the Northern zone. Replace the first Note with:

   > - The results (Table 4 and the text on page 5) say Northern zone farmers ranked high yield first and disease resistance second, while the discussion (page 6) says they treated disease resistance as the most important criterion. Use the results tables.

9. **Add one Note on the women's top criteria (medium).** The text on p. 5 says women ranked post-harvest traits as their highest criteria, but Table 5 (p. 8) ranks market preference, disease resistance and high yield first to third for women. The card's wording avoids the error but does not flag it. Add:

   > - The text says women ranked post-harvest traits highest, but in Table 5 their top three reasons are market preference (87), disease resistance (82) and high yield (79). Post-harvest traits follow at ranks 4 to 7.

10. **Optional additions (medium to low).**
    - Summary, after "where disease resistance came second.": "Farmers preferred the market-favoured Calima Uyole and Uyole 18 less because of their high anthracnose susceptibility." (p. 5). With fixes 2, 6 and 7, the Summary stays near 480 words.
    - Methods, Sampling bullet, add at the end: "The six districts were Karatu and Siha (Northern), Kyerwa and Misenyi (Lake), and Mbarali and Njombe (Southern Highlands)." (p. 2)
    - Key numbers, new row: `| 10–15 vs 2–3 | Detailed trait evaluations per variety in PVS vs comparative rankings per farmer in tricot, the authors' example of tricot's lower data granularity | This paper (illustrative, no source cited) |` (p. 12)
    - Note 4, change "reports log-likelihoods and chi-square probabilities" to "reports log-likelihoods, F values, about 1,086 degrees of freedom and chi-square probabilities" (Table 2, p. 4).
