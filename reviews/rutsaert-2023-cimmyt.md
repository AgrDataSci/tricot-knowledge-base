Almost every statement in the card is traceable to the report, and all six Key numbers rows are correct. But the card is thin. The summary has 276 words, well under the 400 to 500 target. It leaves out several main results: the three market segments with their volume shares, the 74% preference for early maturity, the tricot correlation results, and the low seed access finding (61% neighbours, 31% local markets). It also credits "taste dominates" to farmers, but that figure (69%) comes from the trader survey. The gist repeats this misattribution, and its claim that oil needs "no breeding focus" goes further than the source. The most important fix is to rewrite the summary and gist so the trader, farmer and tricot results are each correctly attributed (pages 10, 26, 28, 29, 34).

## Fixes needed

1. **Summary is too short and misses main results (high).** The card's summary has 276 words. It leaves out the three segments (p. 10), the maturity numbers (p. 29), the tricot results (p. 28), seed access (p. 34) and the low-input testing advice (pp. 41 to 42). It also says "Taste dominates farmers' preferences", but the 69% taste figure comes from the 300 traders (p. 26, footnote "Base: N = 300 Traders", "Source: Accelerate Report"). The card also says "video-based product concept testing", but the report only says "concept testing" (p. 14) and shows no concept test results. Suggested replacement for the whole Summary (475 words):

The report answers research questions raised at the Tanzania groundnut product design team meeting at Naliendele in November 2022. Product design teams are meant to represent the whole value chain, from breeders and seed producers to traders, processors and farmers, and to define market segments and target product profiles. Market intelligence was asked to guide that discussion with evidence on five breeding questions and five seed system questions. The report is a slide deck with descriptive statistics and no formal tests.

It combines four sources. A trader survey covered 300 traders (87 large off-takers and 213 small traders) in 12 regions in May and June 2023. A qualitative study held 20 key informant interviews and 10 focus groups in Dodoma and Shinyanga in October and November 2022. A farmer survey of 1,100 groundnut farmers in Dodoma and Shinyanga in June 2023 included concept testing, but the report shows no concept test results. On-farm tricot testing with 1,203 farmers ran countrywide from 2021 to 2023 under the AVISA and 1000FARMS projects.

The surveyed traders sold 45,207 tons, 68% of it tan. The report groups demand into three segments: small and medium tan (over 45% of volume, mainly Dodoma, used for peanut butter and cooking), large tan (over 20%, mainly Songwe) and large red (under 20%). Drivers differ between groups. Traders most often named taste (69%), oil content (48%), size (37%) and colour (29%) as preference drivers. Size dominates for large groundnuts and oil content for small and medium ones. In the tricot trials, overall preference correlated most strongly with marketability, grain colour and grain size, for both men and women.

Early maturity (90 to 105 days) is preferred by 74% of surveyed farmers in these arid and semi-arid areas, and the authors suggest that later, larger types may suit highland areas. Most farmers (72%) leave crop residue on the field as mulch and only 17% feed it to livestock, so the authors see no case for breeding for fodder. There is no demand for a specialised oil groundnut, though oil content matters for peanut butter. Half of the farmers use no inputs after planting, so the authors recommend testing new varieties under low-input conditions.

For seed systems, access is low. Among 485 farmers asked about seed sources, 61% get seed from neighbours, 31% from local markets and only 7% from out-growers or agro-dealers. Of all 1,100 farmers, 82% recycle seed all the time or most seasons. Yet 92% of the 485 would buy fresh seed if it were available, and 42% every season. Almost half of the traders said their customers plant the grain they buy, and 24% prepare grain to sell as seed, so traders are a possible seed channel. Women do most groundnut field work, know more about varieties and use groundnut as a cash reserve, so they emerge as a key customer group.

2. **Gist misattributes taste and overstates oil (high).** Card: "early maturity and taste drive groundnut demand, fodder and oil need no breeding focus". Taste as the top driver comes from traders (p. 26). The source says there is no demand for a specialised oil groundnut but that oil content matters for peanut butter, and it lists "Oil content requirements for peanut butter" as a target product profile priority for the largest segment (pp. 8, 10). Suggested gist (29 words):

`gist: Trader, farmer and tricot data from Tanzania define three groundnut segments, show farmers prefer early maturity and rarely feed residue, and find low seed access but high purchase interest`

3. **not_established is missing (high).** Suggested text:

`not_established: that taste is the top preference driver for farmers (the 69% figure comes from 300 traders); that the segment shares reflect national production (they come from traders' reported sales); that this report presents concept test results (none are shown); that early maturity is the strongest driver in tricot rankings (in the on-farm data it is among the traits least correlated with overall preference, read off a chart); that farmers would pay for certified seed (purchase interest was a hypothetical question to 485 farmers); that small and medium groundnuts were confirmed as one group in tricot (the tricot slide is not split by product type)`

8. **PDT attendance (low).** Card: "where breeders, seed producers, traders, processors and farmers met". Page 4 says product design teams "should consist of" members from the whole value chain. It does not list who attended. The replacement summary in fix 1 rewords this.

9. **Notes (low).** Add: `The on-farm slide (p. 28) credits its results to Happy Daudi, Kauê de Sousa et al. (2023) and the AVISA and 1000FARMS projects. The trader-survey slides (pp. 20 to 27) give "Accelerate Report" as their source.` Also add the source-issue notes below.

10. **Format (low).** The card has a long-format "Questions this paper answers" section but no "Methods in brief" or "Stated limitations". The report states no limitations of its own. If the card is meant to be long format, add a short Methods section built from pp. 12 to 15, and under Stated limitations write `The report states no limitations. It is a descriptive slide deck with no statistical tests, and the seed questions use a subsample of 485 farmers.`
