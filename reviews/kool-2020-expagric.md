The card is accurate. Every number in the Summary and Key numbers checks out against the text and figures, and nothing is contradicted. The main weaknesses are a Summary that is below the word target (382 words), a few frequency words that are stronger than the source ("many", "usually"), one recommendation credited to the authors that the source credits to Coe et al. (2003), and the absence of the crop-information figures (variety named, planting date given) that matter most to tricot users. The most important fix is to add those figures (p. 596), which also brings the Summary into the 400 to 500 word range.

## Fixes needed

1. **Summary is short and misses the crop-information results (p. 596, PDF p. 10, and p. 595, PDF p. 9).** The Summary has 382 words (target 400 to 500). It does not report that only 69% of studies named the variety, 41% gave the planting date and 10% described all four crop variables. It also does not report the authors' own caveat that a reproducible design does not mean reproducible findings (p. 595). Replace the third Summary paragraph with:

   > To judge reproducibility they scored each paper on 23 yield-determining variables in seven categories based on production ecology (field history, crop information, land preparation, nutrients, water, weeds, pests). The average score was 8.9 of 23 (39%), ranging from 1 to 18. A high score means the design could be repeated, not that the findings would be, since conditions such as rainfall change between seasons. Nutrient management, weed management and crop information were best described. Even so, 69% named the variety, 41% gave the planting date and only 10% described all four crop variables. Pest management was not described at all in 76% of studies, land preparation in 59% and field history in 53%. Only 15% gave the previous crop, and some studies said only that farmers followed their usual practice, without saying what that was.

   This also fixes "many said only that farmers used their usual practice". The source says "Some studies refer solely to 'common farmer practice'" (p. 597).

2. **Recommendation credited to the wrong source (p. 602, PDF p. 16).** The card lists "compare host farmers with the wider population" among the authors' proposals. The source raises it in the textbook section as a check for selection bias "as suggested by Coe et al. (2003)". It is not in the authors' list of proposals (p. 601, p. 603). The card also misses the authors' call for journals to put the proposals into reviewer guidelines (p. 603). Replace the last Summary paragraph with:

   > The authors argue that this undermines the case for on-farm research in an era of "impact at scale". They propose that every study state under what conditions the treatment effect occurs and for whom the findings are relevant, and that journals add this to reviewer guidelines. Studies should be transparent about site and farmer selection, reflect on whether a farmer practice control represents real practice, and report the seven categories of yield-determining factors, possibly in supplementary material. Citing Coe et al. (2003), they note that comparing host farmers with the wider population is one way to check for selection bias.

3. **Farmer practice frequency words are too strong, and the gap in at-scale studies is missing (p. 599 to 600, p. 603).** The card says the farmer practice control was "usually poorly described and treated as uniform". The source says it is "often not clearly described" and that "many studies" assume it is uniform. It gives no percentage. The source also says external validity is poorly addressed "even in studies that use stratified or experimentation at scale approaches" (p. 603). In the fourth Summary paragraph, replace "29% included a "farmer practice" control, usually poorly described and treated as uniform, although farmers' practices vary widely." with:

   > 29% included a "farmer practice" treatment, mostly as the control. It was often poorly described and treated as uniform, although farmers' practices vary widely. The authors see the same gap in the few stratified and at-scale studies.

   With fixes 1 to 3 and fix 4 below, the Summary has 483 words.

4. **"Hotspots near CGIAR centres" is more precise than the source (p. 593, PDF p. 7).** The source says hotspots are in East and West Africa, "notably in countries with substantial presence" of CGIAR institutes. It is about countries, not distance to centres. Replace "with hotspots near CGIAR centres" with:

   > with hotspots in East and West African countries with a strong CGIAR presence

5. **Gist understates the main external-validity finding (p. 598, PDF p. 12).** "few define for whom or where their results hold" should be "none", since no study explicitly defined its research population. Replace the gist with (27 words):

   > Review of 172 on-farm agronomy studies in Africa finds most report too little to be repeated and none explicitly defines for whom or where its results hold

6. **Search bullet skips a step (p. 589, PDF p. 3, and Appendix I, p. 607, PDF p. 21).** "1,005 hits, 744 non-review papers" suggests only reviews were removed. In fact the 1,005 hits were first limited by research area to 767, then reviews were removed to give 744. The exclusion list is also incomplete. Replace the Search bullet with:

   > - **Search.** Web of Science Core Collection, 28 October 2016, topic search for experiments or trials on farms in Africa, 1986-2015. 1,005 hits, 767 after limiting to agricultural and environmental research areas, 744 after removing reviews. 172 retained after excluding studies outside sub-Saharan Africa, on-station or with unclear location, pot or laboratory work, natural experiments, non-agronomy topics, data published elsewhere and set-ups such as rainfall simulators.

7. **Third stated limitation misreads the scoring rule (p. 591 to 592, PDF p. 5 to 6).** The card says the score records whether information is given "not whether the practice was done well". The source says the score is about information given, "not on whether the practice was done". Its example is a study that clearly stated no fertilizer was applied and still received full nutrient points. Quality of practice is not discussed. Replace the bullet with:

   > - The score records whether information is given, not whether a practice was applied. A study that clearly stated no fertilizer was used got full points for nutrients. A full score means the design could be repeated, not that the findings would be, since conditions such as rainfall vary between seasons.

   Also replace the first limitation (which has a semicolon) with:

   > - The search is not exhaustive. A search by country names doubled the hits, but the authors found the same distribution across countries and assume the findings also apply.

   And add:

   > - Annual publication counts (Figure 1) need caution, because later publication records hold more abstracts and keywords and so are more likely to be found.

8. **Notes say "not variety trials", but cultivar studies are included (p. 593 to 594, PDF p. 7 to 8).** 22 of 172 studies had cultivars as their main topic, and 16% of studies varied cultivars between treatments (Table 2). Replace the first sentence of the first Notes bullet with:

   > - Mostly about agronomy trials. Only 22 of 172 studies had cultivars as their main topic, and 16% varied cultivars between treatments.

9. **Key numbers: clarify topic counts and add tricot-relevant rows (p. 593 to 596).** Replace the 75 / 36 / 26 / 22 context with "Studies by main topic: nutrients / pests and weeds / tillage / cultivars (a study can count under more than one, and 34 had other topics)". Add:

   > | 69% / 41% / 10% | Studies naming the variety / giving the planting date / describing all four crop variables | This paper |
   > | 16% | Studies whose treatments differed in cultivar (Table 2) | This paper |

10. **Crops and data fields (see Metadata check).** Replace `crops` with:

    > crops: many crops, not broken down by crop (examples include maize, sorghum, rice, cassava, legumes, potato and vegetables)

    Replace `data` with:

    > data: supplementary materials at the publisher give details of the paper selection (https://doi.org/10.1017/S0014479720000174); no dataset DOI

11. **not_established could be stronger.** Suggested replacement:

    > not_established: that the reviewed experiments gave wrong results (the review scores reporting, not correctness); that a high reproducibility score means the findings would replicate; that more farms alone fix external validity; that the 172 papers cover all on-farm research in Africa (one Web of Science search, 1986-2015); findings for variety trials specifically, since only 22 of 172 studies were mainly on cultivars
