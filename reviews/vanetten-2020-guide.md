The card is accurate on almost all of the guide's numbers (8 to 12 options, 100 to 200 farmers, up to 25 farmers per field agent, groups of 5 to 7, four package elements). The most important fix is one Contradicted sentence: the guide says field agents photograph the observation cards, not the trials (p.23). The card also misnames three of the ten steps, generalizes the 20-farmer limit to all workshops, and leaves out three practical rules a tricot user needs: blind packages, balanced randomization by consecutive package numbers per village, and the limit of ten evaluation criteria.

## Fixes needed

1. **Photos of trials (Contradicted).** Card, Summary paragraph 5: "Field agents photograph trials and record the farmer's name and package code with each photo, and can collect data by calling farmers or a neighbor's phone when visits are not possible." Source p.23 (PDF 13): one alternative is to take photos of the observation cards and copy the data later, noting the farmer's name and package ID with each photo. Calling the farmer on their own or a neighbor's phone is listed as another way to make collection more efficient. The guide does not say "when visits are not possible". Replace with:
   > Field agents can also photograph observation cards, noting the farmer's name and package ID with each photo, or call farmers on their own or a neighbor's phone.

2. **Step names (Imprecise).** Card, Methods: "Planning, design, recruitment, package preparation, execution, observation, data collection, analysis, feedback, evaluation." Source p.5 (PDF 4) and step headings: Preparation, Design, Recruitment, Distribution, Execution, Observation, Compilation, Analysis, Feedback, Evaluation. Packages are prepared in Step 2 (p.13) and handed out in Step 4. Replace with:
   > - **Ten steps.** Preparation, design, recruitment, distribution, execution, observation, compilation, analysis, feedback, evaluation.

3. **Workshop size (Imprecise).** Card, Summary paragraph 3: "Workshops work best with a maximum of 20 farmers." Key numbers: "≤20 farmers | Maximum per workshop". Source p.17 (PDF 10): the maximum of 20 is for the training and distribution workshop, held about four weeks before the trial. For feedback workshops the guide says around 20 to 25 per event (p.25, PDF 14). Replace the summary sentence with:
   > Training and distribution workshops work best with a maximum of 20 farmers, held about four weeks before the trial starts.

   Replace the Key numbers row with:
   > | ≤20 farmers | Maximum per training and distribution workshop (feedback workshops: around 20 to 25) | This guide |

4. **Observation timing (Imprecise).** Card, Summary paragraph 3: "at set moments such as sowing, 30 to 75 days after planting, and after harvest." Source: timing depends on the technology and implementers set it per criterion (p.12, p.21). The text example is day of sowing, after 30 days and day of harvest (p.12). The 45 and 75 days after planting on the observation sheet (p.2) are another, crop-specific example, and that sheet also has collections 5 and 45 days after harvest. Mixing the two makes "30 to 75 days" look like a rule. Replace with:
   > Observation questions ask which option was best and which was worst for one trait at a time, at moments that implementers set for each criterion, for example at sowing, about 30 days later and at harvest. The guide advises no more than ten criteria, always including overall performance and why the farmer prefers the best option.

   And the Methods bullet with:
   > - **Observation.** Best and worst questions, one criterion at a time, at times set by implementers (for example at sowing, about 30 days later and at harvest).

5. **Feedback workshops (Imprecise).** Card: "Feedback workshops in small groups of five to seven people let farmers discuss results and help design the next cycle." Source p.25: the 5 to 7 person groups, with a facilitator, are one part of a workshop of at least half a day. Improving the next cycle is Step 10, where farmers' experiences and complaints feed the evaluation by implementers, researchers and field agents (p.27). Replace with:
   > In feedback workshops, farmers discuss results in small groups of five to seven people with a facilitator, and their comments feed the evaluation that improves the next cycle.

6. **Missing practical rules (high-importance omissions).** Add to the end of Summary paragraph 2:
   > Packages are blind, so farmers learn the names of their options only after data collection. ClimMob balances the randomization, and each village must receive packages with consecutive numbers so that every option is present in every village.

   Source: p.5 (blind), p.17 (balanced randomization and consecutive numbers), p.28 glossary.

7. **Word count after fixes 1 to 6.** These edits bring the summary to about 511 words. Delete the last sentence of paragraph 5 ("The guide stresses that including more farmers in each cycle should be a constant objective, so that more households benefit from the research."), which repeats paragraph 3. The result is about 487 words.

8. **Stated limitations (Imprecise).** The guide has no limitations section. The second bullet turns a deliberate design choice into a limitation. The guide says limited control of conditions is a defining feature, chosen to maximize external validity (p.30), and that both extra care and neglect of trial plots distort results (p.19). Replace both bullets with:
   > - The guide gives no limitations section. Its practical constraints are that each field agent can cover only up to 25 farmers by visits, and that more farmers means more work to assist them (p.7, p.23).
   > - Trial plots must be managed exactly like the farmer's regular plot. Extra care or neglect will distort the results (p.19).
   > - The guide says it can only assist local design, because every tricot project differs by country, crop, farming system and people (p.27).
   > - It recommends tricot only where agronomic innovation can be part of the solution, after a problem analysis (p.6).

9. **Gist (topic list).** Card gist lists topics. Replace with:
   > gist: Practical guide showing that a tricot project runs in ten steps, with 8 to 12 options, blind three-option packages, about 100 to 200 farmers at first, and ClimMob analysis
   (28 words.)

10. **not_established (missing).** Add:
    > not_established: that 100 to 200 farmers is enough for every crop or trait (the guide says most situations); that the 30, 45 or 75 day observation times are fixed rules; that tricot has been validated for technologies other than annual crop varieties (the guide says such trials are underway); that the guide reports any trial results or effect sizes; that ClimMob will stay free for commercial or very large trials

11. **Notes (Not in source).** "A more practical, illustrated successor to steinke-2016-guide" is not stated in the guide, which does not cite earlier guides. Keep it but label it as the card writer's comparison:
    > - Card writer's comparison, not stated in the guide: a more practical, illustrated successor to steinke-2016-guide.

    Also add the source issues below to Notes.
