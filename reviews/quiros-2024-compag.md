Most numbers in the card are correct (users, SUS, UMUX-Lite, sample sizes, Table 1 example, 1000FARMS duration). But the Summary describes ClimMob's three domains with the wrong names and contents, and this is the core structure of a software paper. The Stated limitations are mostly the card's own reading, while the real limitations the authors give (analysis limited to one project, Android and internet needed, one general report) are missing. The gist lists topics, not_established is missing, and the key design properties a tricot user cares about (A-optimality, robustness to lost plots, balance across villages and positions) are left out. All fixes are short text changes.

## Fixes needed

1. **Domain names are wrong (Contradicted, p3 to p5).** The card says ClimMob has a "design domain", a "registration and data collection domain" and an "analysis domain". The source (p3, Fig. 3) names the three domains as (1) Libraries, (2) Planning + Management and (3) Analysis. Technologies and questions are defined in the Libraries domain. Trial design, Participant registration and Data collection are three modules of the Planning + Management domain (p4). Replace the whole second Summary paragraph with:

   > Design and workflow were developed together. The resulting workflow is tricot, where farmers compare three technologies with simple rankings. ClimMob organizes the work in three domains. The Libraries domain holds shared lists of technology options and questions. Where possible, system-wide questions are linked to ontology terms, and ranking traits are linked to Crop Ontology. The Planning + Management domain has three modules. In Trial design, users choose technology options and build a sequentially balanced, approximately A-optimal incomplete block design, for example 10 varieties in packages of three for 20 farmers. In Participant registration and Data collection, field agents use Open Data Kit forms generated by the platform, link each participant to a package by scanning its QR code, and a dashboard tracks progress. In the Analysis domain, R packages on CRAN produce overview reports for trial managers and personalized result sheets for each participant, and climatrends supplies climate indices for Plackett-Luce trees.

   This also fixes the overstated Crop Ontology claim (item 6).

2. **Stated limitations are mostly not the authors' own (p6, p8, p9).** "Usability is average, not high" is the card's reading. The authors say the scores are close to the median and closest to "good" (p8). The real limitations in the source are missing. Replace the section with:

   > - The automated analysis covers one ClimMob project at a time. Combining projects needs R skills (p6).
   > - Data collection with ODK needs field agents with Android phones and at least periodic internet access, so farmers in very remote places cannot yet report directly. USSD, IVR and SMS channels were only planned (p9).
   > - ClimMob produces one general report, not reports tailored to different decision-makers such as breeding programmes and variety release committees (p9).
   > - Advanced users could not yet use their own Excel-designed ODK forms with skip logic (p9).
   > - Integration with breeding platforms such as the Enterprise Breeding System, and with genomic data, was planned but not yet in place (p9).
   > - Usability was close to the median for software (SUS 68.1, closest to "good"), measured with small samples (16 and 25 users), so there is room to improve (p8).

3. **gist lists topics instead of what the paper shows.** Replace with (26 words):

   > gist: ClimMob, open-source software co-designed with the tricot workflow, supports tricot trials from design to automated reports, has close to 800 users and median-level usability (SUS 68.1)

4. **not_established is missing.** Add:

   > not_established: that tricot data collected with ClimMob are more accurate than other methods (this is a software paper and the science is described elsewhere); that ClimMob improves breeding decisions or variety adoption; that the usability scores represent all users (16 and 25 respondents, first quarter of 2022); that integration with EBS, genomic data, FormShare or USSD, IVR and SMS channels existed in 2024 (all planned); that all traits in all projects are linked to Crop Ontology

5. **Planned work sentence is imprecise (p8 to p9).** The card says all planned work continues under 1000FARMS and lists "optional household surveys such as RHoMIS". The source gives four areas. 1000FARMS is named for user experience (6.1) and integration (6.3). RHoMIS is only an example of what users could add once Excel-designed ODK forms are possible through FormShare (6.2). The fourth area, reports tailored to different decision-makers (6.4), is missing. Replace the last sentence of Summary paragraph 4 with:

   > Planned work covers four areas: better user experience, more data collection options (Excel-designed ODK forms through FormShare, for example to add RHoMIS household surveys, and USSD, IVR and SMS channels for remote farmers), integration with breeding platforms such as the Enterprise Breeding System and with genomic data, and reports tailored to different decision-makers. Part of this work runs under 1000FARMS, a 4-year project.

6. **Last Summary sentence is not in the source.** "This fast loop, together with standard trait definitions, is what allows many different organizations to run comparable trials" is the card's own synthesis and is stated more strongly than the source. The source says standardization was needed because trials named traits differently, which blocked aggregation across seasons and trial managers (p3). Replace with:

   > Standard names for technologies and traits were added because trials named traits in slightly different ways, which made it hard to combine data across seasons and trial managers.

7. **Analysis time differs within the source (p2 versus p6).** The card gives about ten minutes (p2). Section 3.3 (p6) says 3 to 7 minutes. In Summary paragraph 5 change "in about ten minutes, depending on dataset size" to:

   > in about ten minutes, depending on dataset size (section 3.3 says 3 to 7 minutes)

8. **Opening sentences are slightly stronger than the source (p1, p2).** "ClimMob is the software that makes tricot workable at scale" and "without specialist staff". The source says implementation at scale requires software (abstract) and that design was kept simple so researchers could run trials "without much external help" (p2). Replace the first two sentences of the Summary with:

   > ClimMob is the software built to support tricot trials at scale. Experimental citizen science needs a workflow that farmers can follow and that gives breeders and advisors relevant results. It also needs software that lets many organizations design trials, collect data and analyze results without much external help.

   With fixes 1 and 5 to 8 together, the Summary is 491 words (checked).

9. **Optional, same word count: add design rationale.** In Summary paragraph 5, the sentence "The team realized that every step of the experimental cycle needed digital support, so workflow and software had to be designed together." repeats paragraph 2. Replace with (p2):

   > Trials are blind, and ranking was chosen over rating because it needs little training and leads to fewer errors.

10. **data field (p11).** The card says "none". The source says data are available on request and has a supplementary appendix describing the database tables. It also cites the analysis workflow and ODK Tools code on Zenodo. Replace with:

    > data: Data available on request (p11). Appendix with database table descriptions at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2023.108539. Analysis workflow code: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.4609907. ODK Tools: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7414915

11. **Key numbers: attribution and missing rows (p6, p7, p8).** The UMUX-Lite to SUS conversion (~70) comes from Borsci et al. 2015. Replace the row and add three rows:

    > | 73.0 (95% CI 6.8) | UMUX-Lite score, 25 users, first quarter of 2022 | This paper |
    > | ~70 | SUS equivalent of the UMUX-Lite score | Conversion from Borsci et al. 2015, cited in this paper |
    > | 68 | Median SUS score for software, so ClimMob is better than about 50% of a reference set | Brooke 2013, cited in this paper |
    > | 3 to 7 min | Time to produce an automated report (p6). Section 2 (p2) says about 10 min | This paper |
    > | 26 | MySQL tables that store ClimMob data | This paper |

    Also change "68.1 (CI 5.1)" to "68.1 (95% CI 5.1)".

12. **Note 3 is outside knowledge, unlabelled and has typos.** The source only shows that co-author Madriz is at MrBot Software Solutions (p1). Replace with:

    > - Outside this paper (checked on the websites, not in the source): ClimMob is currently developed by a consortium of the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (https://alliancebioversityciat.org/tools-innovations/climmob) and MrBot Software Solutions (https://mrbotcr.com), which is in charge of its support and maintenance. In the paper, co-author B. Madriz is affiliated with MrBot.

13. **Add Notes on source issues.** See the Source issues table for ready text.

14. **Keywords (minor).** The source keywords are Crowdsourcing, Decentralized experiments, Digital inclusion and Tricot approach. The card has none of "tricot" or "citizen science". Add "tricot, citizen science" (12 keywords in total).

15. **software field (minor).** Table 2 (p8) also lists chirps. Add "chirps". I could not check the current ClimMobTools version on CRAN because the network proxy blocked CRAN. The paper cites ClimMobTools v0.4.5.
