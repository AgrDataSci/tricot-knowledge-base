The card is accurate on the ten steps, the variety numbers, the seed-bag formula and the Honduras farmer example. The main problem is the list of limitations. The card invents one limitation (preferred varieties not yet available), misreads another, and leaves out the guide's own limitation that farmers may find no tested variety better than their own. The card also promises an answer on participant numbers but never gives the guide's advice of 100 to 200 participants, and it credits the "100 observers, 12 varieties" figure to Steinke et al. 2017, which the guide does not cite.

## Fixes needed

1. **Limitations are misstated (Summary and Stated limitations).**
   The card says: "a large number of participants must work independently, and some may find a variety they like that is not yet available" and, in Stated limitations, "Varieties farmers prefer may not yet be available to them."
   The source (p. 4, PDF 6) lists four limitations: farmers may need training and assistance, which requires investment. There is a risk that some or all participants find none of the tested varieties better than their own. Tricot needs many participants to work well. If too few farmers report back, results may not be useful to researchers, although the trials can still help farmers. Nothing about varieties being unavailable appears. Unreleased varieties are listed as a strength, not a limitation.
   Replace the Summary sentences "It is equally clear about limitations. Farmers may need initial training, some may not report back, a large number of participants must work independently, and some may find a variety they like that is not yet available." with:
   > It is also open about limitations. Farmers may need training and assistance at first, which costs money. Some or all participants may find none of the tested varieties better than their own. The method needs many participants to work well, and if too few report back, the results may be of little use to researchers, although the trials can still help the farmers.

   Replace the whole Stated limitations list with:
   > - Farmers may need training and assistance at first, which requires an investment.
   > - Some or all participants may find none of the tested varieties better than their own.
   > - Tricot needs many participants to work well. If too few farmers report back, the results may not be useful for researchers, although the trials can still be useful to farmers.
   > - Practical caution (p. 28): including all explanatory variables in one analysis can take a very long time and give results that are hard to interpret, so the guide advises adding them one by one.

2. **Attribution of "100 observers, 12 varieties" (Key numbers).**
   The card credits "Steinke et al. 2017, cited in this guide". The guide (p. 9, PDF 11) only says that good experiences came from "a recent study". It gives no reference, and a 2016 guide cannot cite a 2017 paper. Replace the Primary source cell with:
   > Unnamed "recent study" mentioned in this guide (p. 9), no reference given

3. **Participant numbers are promised but not given.**
   Question 2 asks how many participants a first project should include. The guide advises around 100 to 200 participants for a first project, and no more than local facilitators can assist (p. 9, PDF 11). Each local facilitator may handle up to 25 participants (p. 26, PDF 28). In the Summary, after "Start with 8 to 12 varieties.", add:
   > For a first project, it advises around 100 to 200 participants, and never more than the local facilitators can support, with up to 25 farmers per facilitator.

   Add Key numbers rows:
   > | 100–200 | Recommended number of participants for a first project | This guide (p. 9) |
   > | ≤25 | Participants per local facilitator | This guide (p. 26) |

4. **ClimMob does not generate the observation cards (Summary).**
   The card says ClimMob "generates the randomized list of packages, the observation cards and the analysis reports". The source says ClimMob creates the project, the package list (randomization) and a data spreadsheet (p. 16, PDF 18), and the analysis report and info sheets (p. 29, PDF 31). For observation cards, climmob.net offers examples and a template (p. 12, PDF 14), which a graphic designer must adapt locally (glossary, p. 35, PDF 37). Replace the sentence with:
   > ClimMob generates the randomized list of packages, a data spreadsheet, the analysis report and an information sheet for each farmer, which gives the names of their three varieties and the varieties recommended for their farm. Its randomization includes every pair of varieties equally often, so packages should follow its list in order. Observation cards are adapted locally from a template on the ClimMob website.

5. **"First" is outside knowledge (Summary sentence 1 and gist).**
   The guide calls itself "Version 1" (cover) and does not say it is the first tricot manual. Replace "This is the first practical manual for implementing tricot, written for researchers, NGOs and extension organizations." with:
   > This is a practical manual for implementing tricot, written for researchers, extension agents and NGO staff.

   If the curators want to keep the point, add to Notes:
   > - Version 1 of May 2016. The guide does not call itself the first tricot manual. Its early date suggests it is one of the first (curator's note, outside the source).

6. **Gist lists topics instead of what the guide says.** Replace with (27 words):
   > A step-by-step manual, illustrated by a Honduras bean project, advising 8 to 12 varieties, 100 to 200 farmers, three-variety packages and ClimMob for design, analysis and feedback

7. **not_established is missing.** Add:
   > not_established: that the guide's numbers (8 to 12 varieties, 100 to 200 participants, at most 20 farmers per workshop, up to 25 farmers per facilitator) were tested, as they are practical advice; that the Honduras bean case produced published, statistically tested results, as the guide reports it only as a narrative example; that tricot raises adoption rates, which the guide expects but does not measure; that the ClimMob screens, files and apps described still match the current platform

8. **Data bullet credits compilation to facilitators (Methods).**
   Local facilitators collect the data. The implementing organization compiles and analyzes it (p. 5, PDF 7, and p. 26, PDF 28). Replace the bullet with:
   > - **Data.** Observation card filled through the season, collected by local facilitators, then compiled and analyzed in ClimMob by the implementing organization.

9. **Seed calculation numbers missing (Key numbers, Question 3).** The card gives the bag formula but not the rounding step or the worked numbers. The card's own recomputation: 1000 × 3 = 3000 bags, 3000 / 14 = 214.3, rounded up to 215, 215 × 120 g = 25,800 g = 25.8 kg. Bean seed: 600 plants × 0.2 g = 120 g. All match the source. In the Summary, replace "The total number of seed bags equals participants times three, from which bags per variety follow." with:
   > The total number of seed bags equals participants times three. Dividing by the number of varieties and rounding up gives bags per variety, which multiplied by the seed per bag gives the seed needed per variety.

   Add Key numbers rows:
   > | 120 g | Seed per variety per trial in a bean example (six rows of 8 m, about 600 plants at about 0.2 g per seed) | This guide (p. 10) |
   > | 215 bags, 25.8 kg | Worked example: 1000 participants × 3 bags / 14 varieties, rounded up, × 120 g per bag | This guide (p. 11) |

10. **Methods lack planting and evaluation rules.** Add bullets:
   > - **Planting.** Trial next to or within the production plot, on an average spot. All three varieties sown on the same day, side by side in equal rows (A left, B middle, C right), and managed exactly like the farmer's own crop.
   > - **Observation.** One characteristic at a time, best and worst of three, usually at three stages (early growth, later growth, after harvest). Yield and overall performance are always included, with no more than ten characteristics.
   > - **Evaluation.** Five indicators: rate of completed trials and causes of failure, gender ratio, rate of repeat participation, changes in seed choice, and changes in seed and information exchange.

11. **Minor wording.** In the Summary, "an NGO ran a common bean project with 200 farmers" should be "with about 200 farmers" (p. 8, PDF 10). "plants the three varieties next to her own crop" could be "within her own production plot" (p. 23, PDF 25).
