The card is accurate on the design, sample sizes, case facts and limitations, and all key numbers check out. The main problems are wording that is more certain than the source (champions, PABRA's policy role, smaller donors) and two missing points that a tricot user would care about: the authors suggest that champions, not evidence from adopted varieties, move national policy (p. 15), and the paper says PABRA's effect on national policy has never been assessed (p. 9, p. 12). The summary is also short (387 words, target 400 to 500), so these additions fit.

## Fixes needed

1. **Champions sentence is too certain, and the key "champions, not evidence" finding is missing.** The card says change "depends on motivated champions working without institutional buy-in". The source says change is "often driven" by champions, "frequently without" buy-in (p. 15, also abstract p. 1). In the Discussion the authors go further and suggest that vocal champions, and not the evidence from adapted and adopted varieties, change national policy (p. 15). This matters directly for tricot users who expect trial evidence to drive policy. Replace the sentence "Change depends on motivated champions working without institutional buy-in." with:

   > Change is often driven by motivated champions, frequently without institutional buy-in. The authors suggest that these champions, rather than evidence from adapted and adopted varieties, are what move national policy, which raises questions about durability once they leave.

2. **PABRA's policy role is stated without the paper's own caveats.** The card says PABRA "built a culture of accountability" and "helped Uganda legalize quality-declared bean seed". The source says results "strongly suggest" PABRA built accountability to farmers (p. 12), that its farmer engagement did not directly influence farmer participation in policy processes (p. 12), and that there is no assessment of whether PABRA actually changed national policies (p. 9). Uganda: PABRA, with Wageningen's ISSD project, was "a significant actor" in the reform (p. 13). Replace the PABRA sentence with:

   > In eastern Africa, PABRA appears to have built a culture of accountability to farmers in national programmes, and it was a significant actor, with Wageningen's ISSD project, in Uganda's reform that let farmer organizations sell quality-declared bean seed. But it leaned towards market-led priorities, did not directly raise farmer participation in policy processes, and remains constrained by centralized certification. The authors note that no study has assessed whether PABRA actually changed national policies.

3. **Honduras sentence mixes informant views with facts.** The card states as fact that the national breeding programme "now treats participatory approaches as central". This is a quote from one FIPAH informant (p. 10). "Never been accepted" is from one bean breeding respondent (p. 10). "CIAL seed must be sold as grain" is the authors' own statement, not an informant's (p. 10). Replace with:

   > One FIPAH informant says the main national breeding programme now treats participatory approaches as central. Yet a bean breeding respondent says participatory breeding has never been accepted nationally, and the authors note that CIAL seed must still be sold as grain.

4. **Mali sentence misstates who runs the programme.** The card says the programme is "run by IER, ICRISAT and two farmer organizations". The source says breeders came from IER, ICRISAT and IPR, and the programme "assisted two farmer organizations" (ULPC Dioila and COOPROSEM, with AOPP) to coordinate trials and seed production (p. 7 to 8). Replace with:

   > The sorghum programme in Mali, with breeders from IER, ICRISAT and IPR and participatory work since 1998, helped two farmer organizations run farmer-managed trials and cooperative seed production.

5. **Smaller donors overstated as "mostly".** The card says long-term support "came mostly from smaller donors". The source says that in two of the three cases (FIPAH Honduras and Sorghum Mali) the donors belong to an "alternative" group outside the CGIAR Trust Fund list, and that small donors played a disproportionately large role (p. 16). PABRA's founding donors were CIDA, SDC and USAID (p. 9). Replace the last sentence of that paragraph with:

   > Education is the weakest principle in the text, and in two of the three cases (Honduras and Mali) long-term support came from smaller, "alternative" donors outside the CGIAR Trust Fund list.

6. **Partnerships sentence slightly too certain.** The card says "Change happens when" farmer-first organizations partner with more influential ones. The source says change is "more likely" then (p. 15). Replace "Change happens when" with "Change is more likely when".

7. **Gist uses "show" and "far behind".** The source says the case studies "suggest" (p. 15) and that policy "lags behind" (p. 15). PABRA is a bean research and seed network, not only a breeding programme. Replace the gist with (26 words):

   > Three long-running participatory breeding and seed programmes suggest that farmer participation spreads through champions and partnerships, while national seed and research policies lag behind established practice

8. **Data field claims something the paper does not say.** The paper has no data availability statement. It lists the policy documents (Annex 2), the policy review protocol (Annex 1) and the interview protocol (Annex 3) in the supplement (p. 6). It says interviews were recorded and transcribed (p. 6) but says nothing about sharing them. Replace with:

   > data: policy document list (Annex 2), policy review protocol (Annex 1) and interview protocol (Annex 3) in the supplementary materials, but the paper has no data availability statement and does not share interview transcripts

9. **Case selection bullet.** "More than a decade" should be "at least a decade" (p. 5). The link to Farmer First workshops or books is a shared feature the authors note after the criteria, and two other features (extensive policy engagement, a body of peer-reviewed evidence) are missing. Replace with:

   > - **Case selection.** Plant breeding or seed system projects, geographically diverse, with sustained exposure to farmer-first principles, run at national or regional scale for at least a decade. All three also engaged extensively with policy actors, had a body of peer-reviewed evidence, and had lead researchers who took part in the Farmer First workshops or the 2009 book.

10. **USAID note is ambiguous.** "The USAID funding" could be read as PABRA's USAID funding (USAID is a PABRA founding donor, p. 9). The source says this study was funded under USAID Cooperative Agreement 7200AA-19LE-00005, and that support ended on 27 February 2025 when the project was cancelled (p. 17). Replace with:

    > - This study was funded by USAID (Cooperative Agreement 7200AA-19LE-00005). The paper says this support ended on 27 February 2025, when the project was cancelled, which falls inside the January to April 2025 interview period.

11. **Optional additions for completeness** (see Completeness table): the definition of policy that includes uncodified practice, the Fig. 2 ratings, the not_established additions, and a Notes entry on the source issues.
