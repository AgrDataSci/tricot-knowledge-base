The card is accurate on most points and the cost figures are taken from the right passage. The main problem is how the ~40% is framed. The source gives it as a rough, unsourced estimate for tricot against conventional PVS when both are run intensively, and the card's Notes say it "matches" a later Central America estimate that, by the knowledge base's own desousa-2024-asd card, is against RCBD and reportedly 28% vs group PVS in its primary source. The card also gives a one-sided picture of plot size, omits the gender finding, and has no not_established.

## What the paper says about cost (requested check)

All cost statements are in section 13, printed p. 21 (PDF p. 26). Nothing on cost appears elsewhere except a passing mention on p. 2 that digital tools help reduce costs and on p. 6 that excluding participants raises relative costs.

1. Cost reduction is called a possible benefit that could drive adoption.
2. The authors say precise cost comparisons are difficult because there is no gold standard to compare with. Both conventional PVS and tricot can be implemented in different ways.
3. **The ~40%:** if both conventional participatory variety selection and tricot are run in a very intensive way (organizing farmer groups, meetings, working with RTB seed materials), the cost reduction is "estimated to be roughly 40%". So the comparator is conventional PVS, both run intensively, in an RTB planting-material context. It is a reduction, so tricot would cost about 60% of PVS.
4. The paper gives no data, no method, no citation, no country, no cost unit (per trial, per farm or per data point) and no named estimator for the 40%. It is an unsourced author estimate.
5. **The tenfold:** "at the other extreme", a tenfold cost reduction is possible in the US, where farmers receive seed by mail and use smartphones. The comparator is not restated. The sentence structure implies the same baseline (conventional PVS), but this is not explicit. No source is given (SeedLinked is named later on the page as a US firm using tricot, but not as the source of the figure).
6. One reason for the reduction is that some costs are externalized to farmers, who use their own labor, land and inputs. So the savings are on the implementer's side, not total costs.
7. This raises the question of whether farmers' motivation justifies their investment. Earlier work found motivation is mainly about access to seed and information (Beza et al. 2017).
8. Cost and motivation analyses for Rwanda and Ghana were underway, expected in 2021.
9. Further reductions are possible if farmer networks are kept over time, serviced through channels used for other purposes (such as credit), and used for several crops. Trials could be outsourced to farmer-facing organizations. US business models (Farmer Business Network, FIRST, SeedLinked) are named, and SeedLinked uses tricot.

Not in this paper: any comparison with RCBD, any Central America figure, any reference to Occelli et al.

## Fixes needed

1. **Notes, ~40% "matches" desousa-2024-asd (Occelli et al. 2024).** This paper's ~40% is against conventional PVS run intensively, in an RTB context, with no source (p. 21). The desousa-2024-asd card records its ~40% as against RCBD in Central America, and says the cited primary source reportedly gives 28% vs group PVS. The two are different comparisons and should not be presented as matching. Replace the note with:
   "- The ~40% here is a rough estimate with no data, method, country or cost unit (p. 21). It compares tricot with conventional PVS when both are run intensively, with farmer groups, meetings and RTB planting material. It is not the same comparison as the ~40% vs RCBD for Central America stated in desousa-2024-asd, whose cited source (Occelli et al. 2024) reportedly gives 28% vs group PVS. Do not treat the two figures as confirming each other."

2. **Summary, cost paragraph.** "intensive implementations of PVS and tricot compare at roughly 40% savings for tricot, and up to tenfold savings are possible where seed is mailed and farmers use smartphones, partly because farmers contribute land and labor." The source hedges ("estimated", "roughly"), places the tenfold in the US and lists labor, land and inputs (p. 21). Replace with:
   "On cost, the paper gives only rough estimates, without data or method. If conventional participatory variety selection (PVS) and tricot are both run intensively, with farmer groups, meetings and RTB planting material, tricot is estimated to cost roughly 40% less. At the other extreme, a tenfold reduction is possible in the US, where seed is mailed and farmers use smartphones. Part of the saving comes from shifting costs to farmers, who give their own labor, land and inputs, and farmers mainly join for access to seed and information."

3. **Key numbers, cost rows.** Replace the ~40% and 10× rows with:
   "| ~40% | Estimated cost reduction of tricot vs conventional PVS when both are run intensively (farmer groups, meetings, RTB planting material). No data, method or cost unit given | This paper (unsourced estimate) |"
   "| Up to 10× | Cost reduction possible in the US, where seed is mailed and farmers use smartphones. Comparator not restated | This paper (unsourced estimate) |"

4. **Add not_established** (missing). Suggested:
   "not_established: a measured or audited 40% saving (it is a rough estimate with no data, method or cost unit); a 40% saving against RCBD or in a named country; a tenfold saving outside the US mail-and-smartphone model; that 100 to 200 farms suffice for any trial (the figure is for about 12 entries, comes from one bean study, and more environments need more farms); that men and women do not differ in variety preferences (no differences were detected, but analyses lacked intersectional data); that farmer volumetric yield estimates are validated beyond one common bean study in Kenya"

5. **Summary, plot size.** "On trial dimensions, it defends small plots and many farms." The abstract says plots should be as small as possible "yet avoiding excessive interplot competition", and p. 3 calls neighbor and border effects a valid concern. Replace with:
   "On trial dimensions, it argues for plots as small as possible on many farms, while avoiding interplot competition." and after the regression-fallacy sentence add: "Neighbor and border effects remain a valid concern, so plot size should follow biological knowledge of each crop, and border rows can be omitted. A position (order) effect for the PlackettLuce package was planned for 2021."

6. **Summary, gender.** Add after "Trials must sample the gender and social diversity of users ...": "So far no tricot analysis had found statistically detectable differences between men and women. The authors propose moving beyond sex disaggregation, with a short RHoMIS household survey and purposive recruitment of local task groups, as IITA did for cassava." (p. 5 to 7)

7. **Summary, last paragraph (repeats ethics and data standardization, and contains one claim not in source).** "cassava trait definitions such as root yield ... linked to Crop Ontology" is not in the source. Table 1 (p. 18) is a standardized ranking-question list for ClimMob from expert discussion. The paper says the tricot ranking method still "needs to be included" in Crop Ontology (p. 17). Replace the whole paragraph with:
   "Practical advice includes giving each variety the same number of seeds, not the same weight, so small-seeded varieties are not favored. For consumer testing of sweetpotato, central testing gave data quickly and agreed with home testing on the top varieties, and a minimum relative regret score is proposed to combine farmer and consumer scores. Cost and motivation analyses for Rwanda and Ghana were reported to be under way, with results expected in 2021."

8. **Summary, ClimMob sentence.** "The paper describes ClimMob's recent features, data standardization through Crop Ontology with cassava trait examples, and the R packages for analysis." Replace with:
   "The paper describes ClimMob's recent features, a standardized cassava trait list for ClimMob (Table 1), plans to link tricot traits to Crop Ontology, and the R packages for analysis."

9. **Summary, first paragraph.** "with a focus on root, tuber and banana crops". The paper has no banana content. Examples are cassava, sweetpotato and potato, plus common bean and wheat. Replace with "with examples mainly from cassava, sweetpotato and potato".

10. **Methods, Data bullet.** Written as if standards exist. The source uses "should" and "will" (p. 13, 17). Replace with:
   "- **Data.** Proposed: link ClimMob trait lists to Crop Ontology, standardize trial metadata, and automate anonymized data publication."

11. **Key numbers, 50 kg row.** The figure is an example batch for local cassava processing, given in a passage that cites Teeken et al. 2020 (p. 4). Replace with:
   "| 50 kg | Example batch of cassava product for local food processing in Nigeria, which sets a minimum plot size | This paper, citing Teeken et al. 2020 |"

12. **Key numbers, add rows** (optional, useful to tricot users):
   "| r = 0.98 | Correlation of farmer volumetric (250 ml tin) and researcher yield estimates, common bean, Kenya, all seasons and sites pooled. Agreement fell for differences under 0.5 t/ha | Ochieng et al. 2019, cited in this paper |"
   "| 6 m², 20 vine cuttings | Sweetpotato tricot plot in Ghana, vs 30 m² and 100 cuttings recommended for on-farm trials | This paper, with the recommendation from Grüneberg et al. 2019 |"
   "| ≥6 rows | Minimum wheat plot width for accurate yield, after a 10% bias with 4-row plots | Rebetzke et al. 2014, cited in this paper |"

13. **Frontmatter fixes.** article: "RTB Working Paper No. 2021-2, December 2020". crops: "cassava, sweetpotato, potato (examples), with examples also from common bean and wheat". gist (28 words): "Synthesis of tricot methods: small plots on 100 to 200 farms for about 12 entries, rankings plus farmer yield measures, inclusive sampling, and an estimated 40% saving over intensive PVS". keywords: 13 terms, drop one (for example "traditional knowledge", which is covered by "ethics").

14. **Notes, DOI.** The card says the DOI in the PDF belongs to another work. I could not check this (no web access). The printed DOI 10.4160/23096586RTBWP20212 (p. ii) matches this paper's series number 2021-2, so the claim looks doubtful. Suggest: "- Grey literature. The PDF prints DOI 10.4160/23096586RTBWP20212 (p. ii). `doi` holds the CGSpace handle instead." The curator should confirm whether the DOI fails to resolve or resolves to another work, and add that reason.
